How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $160; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from battery included (30%), wide path (30%), a brushless motor (20%), long throw (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Snow shovel or snow blower?

A cordless snow shovel is a handheld auger 12-16 in wide that you push like a shovel. It suits steps, decks, walks and a short driveway after 6-8 in of snow. A walk-behind blower (16-24 in) rolls on wheels and clears a two-car driveway much faster.

Single-stage or two-stage?

A single-stage blower uses one auger to pick up and throw snow, and it scrapes down to the pavement. The single-stage picks here are rated for 8-10 in. A two-stage machine adds an impeller, so it handles deep, wet and plowed-in snow, but it's heavier and costs far more.

How much runtime do I need?

Listed runtimes on this page range from 15 to 30 minutes on the included batteries; EGO and WORX quote driveway sizes instead. Wet snow drains a battery faster than powder. If your driveway takes longer than one pack, buy into a battery system where a spare is easy to find.

Does the battery come with it?

Not always. The EGO SNS1200 and Greenworks Gen 2 are bare tools for people who already own those batteries. Every other pick on this page includes a battery and charger.

What do clearing width and depth mean?

Width is the path cut in one pass. Depth is the deepest snow the intake handles. Snow deeper than the listed depth has to be taken in two passes, so match depth to your usual storm.
