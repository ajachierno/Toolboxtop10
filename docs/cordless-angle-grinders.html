Buyer's guide

Bare tool or kit?

A bare tool ships with no battery or charger — worth it if you already own the brand's batteries, useless out of the box if you don't. A kit includes batteries, a charger, and usually a set of wheels. Each pick above says which.

4-1/2" or 5" wheel?

4-1/2" is the standard size for most cutting, grinding, and polishing around the house and shop. A grinder rated for 4-1/2" to 5" gives you the option to size up for masonry or heavier metal work later without buying a second tool.

Brushless or brushed?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and hold speed better under load — worth it if you'll use the grinder often or push it hard. For occasional cutting and grinding, a brushed tool like the CRAFTSMAN or RYOBI bare tools is fine.

What does a kickback brake or E-Clutch actually do?

A grinder wheel that binds in a cut can kick the tool back at you hard. A kickback brake or electronic clutch senses that bind and cuts power or stops the wheel in a fraction of a second. It's a real safety feature, not a marketing bullet — worth prioritizing if you'll be cutting metal regularly.

Which battery platform should I buy into?

Buy on the same platform as tools you already own (DEWALT 20V MAX, Milwaukee M18, Makita 18V LXT, CRAFTSMAN V20, RYOBI ONE+) so one battery lineup covers everything. If you own nothing yet, a self-contained kit like the AOBEN or SEESII avoids the platform lock-in.

How many RPM do I need?

Most cordless angle grinders run 8,500-10,000 RPM, which is plenty for cutting, grinding, and polishing metal, tile, and masonry. A variable-speed tool like the Milwaukee 2888-20 lets you slow down for delicate work like paint removal or polishing, where full speed can gouge the surface.
