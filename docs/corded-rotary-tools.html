How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $25; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from electronic speed (30%), flex shaft (25%), big kit (25%), keyless chuck (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 20,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Do I need a Dremel, or is a budget kit fine?

For occasional crafts and cleanup, a $22-40 kit works. Dremel's 4000 and 4300 add electronic speed control that holds rpm when you push on a bit, and the widest range of attachments. If you'll cut metal regularly, that matters.

What speed do I need?

High speeds (25,000-35,000 rpm) for cutting and grinding metal; low speeds (5,000-15,000 rpm) for polishing, plastics and wood carving, where high rpm melts or burns. A low minimum speed is as useful as a high maximum.

What is a flex shaft for?

A flex shaft moves the bit to a pen-sized handpiece on a cable, so you can engrave and carve with fine control while the motor hangs nearby. Hang the tool so the shaft runs in a gentle curve, not a sharp bend.

Collets or a keyless chuck?

Collets grip one shank size each, so you swap collets for different bits. A 3-jaw keyless chuck takes any shank in its range without tools. Collets run truer; chucks are faster.

What safety gear do I need?

Eye protection always: cut-off wheels shatter. Use a dust mask for sanding and grinding, and keep loose sleeves and long hair clear of the bit.
