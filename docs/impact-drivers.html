The 10 Best Impact Drivers

Ranked from live Amazon data — the tool that drives long screws and lag bolts a drill can't.

An impact driver is the tool you reach for when a drill starts to stall: it adds rotational hammer blows to power long screws, deck fasteners, and lag bolts with almost no arm strain, all through a quick-change 1/4-inch hex. DEWALT dominates this category on Amazon, so the list leans that way, but there are strong value picks too. One thing to watch, and the biggest gotcha here: many impact drivers sell as a bare tool (no battery), which is great if you already own a battery platform and a trap if you don't. Buyer's guide

Impact driver vs drill — which do I need?

A drill bores holes and drives small screws with steady torque and a keyed or keyless chuck. An impact driver adds rotational hammer blows to drive long screws, deck fasteners, and lag bolts without stalling or twisting your wrist, using a quick-change 1/4-inch hex. Most people eventually want both; if you only drive fasteners, an impact driver is the one.

Tool only or kit?

This is the biggest trap in impact-driver listings. A 'bare tool' or 'tool only' unit ships with no battery or charger — a bargain if you already own that brand's batteries, and useless out of the box if you don't. A 'kit' includes a battery and charger. Every pick above says which it is.

How much torque do I need?

Impact drivers are rated in inch-pounds. Around 1,400 to 1,800 in-lbs handles nearly all home work: decking, framing screws, and lag bolts. Over 1,800 in-lbs is pro territory for heavy or repetitive driving. All the picks here clear the home-use bar.

Brushless or brushed?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and squeeze more drives out of a charge. They cost more. For occasional projects a brushed unit is fine; if you'll use it weekly, pay up for brushless.

Will my drill's batteries fit?

Only within the same brand and platform. A DEWALT 20V MAX battery fits DEWALT 20V tools, a RYOBI ONE+ battery fits RYOBI ONE+ tools, and so on. Buying an impact driver on the same platform as a drill you already own means one battery lineup for both — a real reason to stay in one brand.

GEVEELIFE

GEVEELIFE 450 N·m 1/2" Impact Wrench

4.4 284 reviews   $49.99

This isn't an impact driver at all — it's a 1/2-inch impact wrench for lug nuts and bolts, with "impact driver" jammed into the title to catch your search. Wrong tool for driving screws.

Why we'd skip it

  • It's an impact wrench, not an impact driver. The business end is a 1/2-inch square drive for sockets (lug nuts, bolts) — not the 1/4-inch hex that holds screwdriver and drill bits. Your impact-driver bits won't fit it.
  • 450 N·m (332 ft-lbs) is automotive torque, built for lug nuts and heavy bolts. Point that at a wood screw and you'll snap the screw or strip the head instantly.
  • The listing calls itself an "impact driver," an "impact wrench," and an "impact gun" on the same page to show up in every search. When a product can't decide what it is, it's fishing for the wrong buyer.
  • If you genuinely need to pull lug nuts or big bolts, an impact wrench is the right tool — but that's a different job from everything else on this list, which drives screws and fasteners.
