How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $140; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from track included (30%), variable speed (25%), depth 2 (25%), cordless (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Why a track saw?

A track saw rides on a straight rail for long, splinter-free cuts in plywood and doors, without hauling sheets onto a table saw. It's the best tool for breaking down sheet goods in a small shop.

Is the track included?

Often not. Check the box contents: Makita's SP6000J1, Festool's TS 55 kit and the Evolution include a track; most others sell it separately. Rails add to the total cost.

Corded or cordless?

Corded saws are cheaper and never run out. Cordless saws from Makita, Milwaukee and WEN go anywhere, which pros value on site.

Will other brands' tracks fit?

Makita and Festool rails are largely interchangeable. DEWALT and WEN use their own. Buy the track that matches your saw.

What blade?

A 48-tooth blade or finer gives clean plywood edges. Use fewer teeth for fast rips in solid wood.
