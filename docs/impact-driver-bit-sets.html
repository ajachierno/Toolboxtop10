How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $20; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from torsion zone (40%), magnetic holder (25%), nut drivers (20%), drill bits (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Do I need impact-rated bits for an impact driver?

Yes, if you drive more than a few screws. An impact driver delivers torque in rapid hammer blows, and a standard bit's hard tip cracks under them. Impact-rated bits use tougher steel (S2 or similar) and usually a narrowed torsion zone that twists slightly to absorb each blow.

What is a torsion zone?

A thinner section of the bit's shaft between the tip and the hex. It flexes a little on each impact so the tip doesn't take the full shock. DEWALT calls it FlexTorq, Wiha MaxxTor, Makita Impact XPS. Bits without one still work but wear out faster under heavy use.

Which bits wear out first?

Phillips #2 (PH2) and square #2 for most people, since they drive deck, drywall and cabinet screws. A set with several PH2 bits, like the Amazon Basics 42-piece with ten, saves a trip to the store.

Do impact bit sets work in a regular drill?

Yes. Every set here uses a 1/4 inch hex shank, which fits a drill's three-jaw chuck as well as an impact driver's quick-release collet. The reverse is not true: round-shank bits won't lock into an impact driver.

Is a 100-plus piece set worth it?

Only if you need the variety. Big sets pad the count with odd sizes and small precision bits you may never use. If you mostly build decks or hang cabinets, a 35 to 60 piece set with good PH2 and square bits plus a magnetic holder covers the work.
