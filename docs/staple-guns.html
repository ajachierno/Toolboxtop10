How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $15; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from power adjust (25%), brads (25%), staples (25%), long warranty (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

T50 or JT21 staples?

T50 is the heavy-duty standard (Arrow's design, made by many brands) in 1/4 to 9/16 inch, for insulation, wrap, upholstery and wood. JT21 is a lighter staple for crafts, fabric and screens. Buy the gun for the staples you'll use.

Do I need a multi-fastener staple gun?

Guns that also drive brads or narrow staples are handy for trim and furniture repair. If you only staple insulation or fabric, a plain T50 is simpler and lasts longer.

What does power adjustment do?

It sets how hard the staple drives. Turn it down for soft materials and fabric so staples don't punch through; up for hardwood.

Manual or electric?

Manual staplers are cheap, reliable and need no power, fine for a project or two. For hundreds of staples a day, an electric or pneumatic stapler saves your hand.

How do I staple over cables safely?

Use a gun with a wire guide and staples sized for the cable, and never pierce the cable jacket. Low-voltage wire only; follow code for electrical wiring.
