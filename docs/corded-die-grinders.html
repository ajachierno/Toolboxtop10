How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $70; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from variable speed (35%), a paddle switch (35%), name brand (30%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,200 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What is a die grinder for?

Small, fast work with 1/4 in shank accessories: carbide burrs for porting and shaping metal, mounted stones for deburring, sanding rolls, and small cutoff wheels for weld cleanup. It spins far faster than an angle grinder but removes less material per pass.

Paddle switch or slide switch?

A paddle switch stops the motor the moment you let go, which matters on a tool that can grab and pull if a burr catches. A slide or rocker switch is easier on the hand during long porting jobs. Some shops require no-lock-on paddles, which is why DEWALT sells the DWE4887N.

Do I need variable speed?

Carbide burrs work best at full speed, but polishing, sanding rolls and plastics need lower speeds to avoid burning. If you only deburr steel, a single-speed grinder is fine. For mixed work, the DWE4997VS, SENCAN and AOBEN all adjust speed.

Corded, air or cordless?

Air die grinders are cheap and light but need a compressor that can keep up. Cordless models are handy away from power but cost more once you add batteries. A corded grinder runs all day at full power with nothing else to buy.

What does AC/DC capability mean?

The DEWALT and Makita AC/DC models can run off the DC output of a welder or a generator, so a welder can grind on site from the same machine.

How do I match accessories to the grinder?

Every burr, stone and wheel has a maximum safe rpm printed on it or its package. It must be at or above the grinder's top speed, which is why a listing that gives conflicting speeds is a problem.
