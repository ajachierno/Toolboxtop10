How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from selective (30%), long warranty (30%), light (20%), a carrying case (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

15-gauge or 16-gauge?

16-gauge nails are thinner and leave smaller holes, fine for baseboard, casing and most trim. 15-gauge nails are thicker and hold better in heavy crown, door jambs and hardwood, and angled 15-gauge magazines fit into corners.

Finish nailer or brad nailer?

Brad nailers shoot thin 18-gauge brads for light trim, small moldings and crafts. Finish nailers drive heavier 15 or 16-gauge nails up to 2-1/2 inches, long enough to hold casing and baseboard into the studs.

What size compressor do I need?

Finish nailers use little air. A small pancake or hot-dog compressor that holds 90 PSI keeps up with one nailer easily.

What are DA and FN nails?

Angled 15-gauge nailers use either DA (34°) or FN (25°) style nails, and they aren't interchangeable. Check which your nailer takes before buying nails.

Bump fire or sequential?

Sequential fires one nail per trigger pull for precise placement. Bump fire shoots each time the nose is pressed while the trigger is held, which is faster on long runs. A switch lets you choose.
