How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $80; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from rack pinion (30%), angle fence (20%), dust collection (20%), cordless (15%), biscuits included (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What does a biscuit joiner do?

It cuts matching crescent slots in two boards. You glue in a compressed wooden biscuit, which swells and locks the joint and keeps edges aligned. It's fast for tabletops, cabinet carcasses and face frames.

Which biscuit sizes?

#0, #10 and #20 cover most work; use the largest that fits the board. Most joiners have preset stops for all three.

Why does the fence matter?

The fence sets how far the slot is from the face. A rack-and-pinion fence stays parallel to the blade, so slots line up on both boards. A tilting fence handles mitered corners.

Corded or cordless?

Corded is cheaper and has unlimited runtime. Cordless DEWALT and Makita joiners are handy for site work and assembly in place.

Biscuits or dowels or dominos?

Biscuits are quickest and forgiving for alignment. Dowels and Festool Domino tenons are stronger for chairs and frames under heavy load.
