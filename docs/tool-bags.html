Buyer's guide

Open tote or zippered bag?

An open-top tote (like the Stanley) is fastest for grab-and-go work and standing tools upright, but offers no protection if it tips or rains. A zippered bag keeps everything in during a carry and shields tools from dust and light weather. Pick open for speed, zip for transport and protection.

Why does a hard molded base matter?

Fabric-bottom bags soak up water from a wet floor and sag under heavy tools. A molded rubber or plastic base keeps the bottom dry, helps the bag stand on its own, and takes the abrasion of being dragged across concrete. It's worth paying for if the bag lives on job sites.

How many pockets do I actually need?

More pockets isn't always better — a 50-pocket bag is great for an electrician with many small tools but wasteful for someone carrying a drill and a few wrenches. Match pocket count to your tool set; a big open bay matters more than pocket count if you carry power tools.

Should I buy into a bag system?

Milwaukee PACKOUT and similar bags have molded bases that lock onto rolling boxes and stack in a tower. That's genuinely useful if you already own the system — otherwise you're paying the system premium for a plain bag. Buy the standalone bag on its own merits unless you're committed to the platform.

What fabric denier should I look for?

Denier (D) measures fabric thickness. 600D is fine for a light-duty tote; 1200D–1680D ballistic fabric resists tears and abrasion for daily jobsite use. Higher denier costs more and adds a little weight, but it's the difference between a bag that lasts one season and one that lasts years.
