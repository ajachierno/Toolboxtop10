How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 50% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 15% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $90; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a complete kit (35%), an electric blade brake (25%), a laser guide (20%), a high bevel capacity (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 60,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Bare tool or kit?

This is the biggest trap in cordless saw listings. A 'bare tool' or 'tool only' saw ships with no battery or charger — a bargain if you already own that brand's packs, useless out of the box if you don't. A 'kit' includes a battery and charger. Every pick above says which it is.

Which battery platform?

A cordless saw is only as good as its batteries. Buying one on the same platform as a drill or driver you already own (DEWALT 20V MAX, Milwaukee M18, RYOBI ONE+, CRAFTSMAN V20) means one battery lineup for everything — a real reason to stay in one brand.

Will cordless keep up with corded?

For most cuts, yes — especially brushless 7-1/4" saws like the DEWALT DCS590. But a corded 15-amp saw still gives more sustained power for the money on long rips and thick stock. If you don't need portability, corded is cheaper and stronger.

What blade size?

6-1/2 inch is the common cordless size — light, with enough depth for 2x lumber in one pass. 7-1/4 inch cordless saws match corded cut depth but drain batteries faster. 4-1/2 inch minis are for plywood, trim, and flooring, not framing.

Watch out for mini saws

Plenty of cheap 'mini' cordless saws in the search results have 4-1/2" or smaller blades and can't cut through a 2x4. They're fine for sheet goods and flooring, but don't buy one expecting a full-size saw — and steer clear of grinders dressed up as saws.

Bigheimer

Bigheimer 4" "Mini Circular Saw" / Cut-Off Tool

This isn't a circular saw — it's a 4-inch angle grinder with a saw guard bolted on. It cuts 5/8 of an inch deep and spins at grinder speed. Wrong, and genuinely unsafe, for the work a circular saw does.

Why we'd skip it

  • It cuts only 5/8 inch (16 mm) deep. A real circular saw cuts about 2-1/2 inches — enough to crosscut a 2x4 in one pass. At 5/8", it can't cut through standard lumber at all.
  • It's a 4-inch angle grinder with a saw guard, spinning at 12,000 RPM. Real circular saws run around 5,000-5,800 RPM; a wood blade at grinder speed is a kickback and injury risk.
  • No-name "Bigheimer" brand, sold tool-only (needs a DEWALT battery), and the lowest star rating in the category at 3.5.
  • If you want to cut metal or tile, buy a grinder. To cut wood, buy a real circular saw.
