The 10 Best Circular Saws

Ranked from live Amazon data — corded and cordless, from budget legends to pro worm drives.

A circular saw is the workhorse that rips sheet goods and crosscuts framing lumber. The big choice is corded versus cordless: corded saws give you full, unlimited power for the least money and never need a battery, while cordless saws trade a little power for the freedom to cut anywhere. DEWALT and SKIL dominate this category on Amazon, and a couple of names — the SKIL 5280 and the SKILSAW worm drive — are near-legendary. Two things to watch: most cordless saws sell as a bare tool (no battery), and plenty of cheap "mini" saws in the results can't actually cut through a 2x4. Each pick below says exactly what it is.

Buyer's guide

Corded or cordless?

Corded saws give you unlimited full power for the lowest price and never need a battery — best for a fixed shop or long cutting sessions. Cordless saws let you cut anywhere with no cord, at a small cost in sustained power and usually a bare-tool price (no battery). If you already own a brand's batteries, a cordless saw on that platform is a easy add.

What blade size do I need?

7-1/4 inch is the full-size standard — it cuts through 2x lumber in a single pass and is what most corded saws use. 6-1/2 inch is common on cordless saws: lighter, slightly shallower. 5-1/2 inch and smaller are compact saws for plywood, trim, and sheet goods, not framing.

Amps vs volts — how much power?

Corded saws are rated in amps; 15 amps is full power, 13 amps is a small step down that's fine for occasional use. Cordless saws are rated in volts (18V/20V MAX) — the battery and motor matter more than the number. Don't compare amps to volts directly.

Sidewinder vs worm drive?

A sidewinder (in-line) saw is lighter and cheaper, with the motor beside the blade — what most people should buy. A worm-drive saw gears the motor for more torque and a front-heavy balance pros like for rip cuts and wet lumber; heavier and pricier.

Does an electric brake matter?

An electric brake stops the spinning blade in a second or two when you release the trigger, instead of letting it coast. It's a real safety and convenience feature — worth looking for, and one reason the DEWALT DWE575SB is our top all-around pick.

Why we'd skip it

  • It cuts only 5/8 inch (16 mm) deep. A real circular saw cuts about 2-1/2 inches — enough to crosscut a 2x4 in one pass. At 5/8", this can't cut through standard lumber or plywood on edge at all.
