Buyer's guide

Bare tool or kit?

A bare tool ships with no battery or charger — worth it if you already own the brand's batteries, a dead end if you don't. A kit includes a battery and charger and sometimes blades, ready to cut out of the box. Each pick above says which.

What does orbital action actually do?

Orbital action moves the blade slightly forward on the upstroke in addition to up-and-down, which speeds up cutting in soft materials like wood at the cost of a rougher edge. Dial it down (or off) for clean cuts in metal, plastic, or finished surfaces, and up for fast, aggressive cuts in framing lumber.

How many strokes per minute (SPM) do I need?

Most cordless jigsaws run 2,500-3,200 SPM at full speed, which covers wood, plastic, and thin metal. Variable-speed triggers let you slow down for tight curves or delicate materials, so the max number matters less than having good low-end control.

Brushless or brushed?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and hold speed better under load — worth it if you'll use the saw often or push it through thick material. For occasional cuts around the house, a brushed tool like the CRAFTSMAN or BLACK+DECKER picks is fine.

Which battery platform should I buy into?

Buy on the same platform as tools you already own (DEWALT 20V MAX, Milwaukee M18, Makita 18V LXT, CRAFTSMAN V20) so one battery lineup covers everything. If you own nothing yet, a self-contained kit like the BLACK+DECKER, WORX, or SEESII avoids the platform lock-in.

T-shank or U-shank blades?

T-shank is the modern standard and what most tool-free blade clamps are designed around — it's what you'll find on hardware store shelves. A saw that accepts both T and U shank blades, like the CRAFTSMAN picks, gives you more flexibility if you're using older or off-brand blades.
