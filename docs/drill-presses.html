How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $130 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from variable speed (30%), a laser guide (25%), light (25%), digital readout (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

What does swing mean on a drill press?

Swing is twice the distance from the column to the center of the chuck. An 8-inch swing can drill the center of an 8-inch-wide board; a 12-inch swing handles 12 inches.

How much spindle travel do I need?

Spindle travel is how deep you can drill in one plunge. 2 inches covers most hobby and metal work. For deep holes in table legs or thick stock, look for 3 inches or more, like the JET JWDP-12.

Variable speed or belt speeds?

Belt-speed presses work fine, but you open the top and move a belt to change speed. Variable-speed presses change with a lever or dial, which matters when you switch between small bits, Forstner bits and metal often.

What speed should I drill metal at?

Slower than wood, and slower still for bigger bits. Large bits in steel want the bottom of the range (500-700 rpm), so a low minimum speed matters more than a high maximum.

Should I bolt a benchtop drill press down?

Yes. They're top-heavy, and JET's listing says its press must be bolted down. Bolt it to a sturdy bench or stand before use.
