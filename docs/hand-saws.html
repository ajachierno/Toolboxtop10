How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $10 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from triple ground (35%), double edge (35%), replaceable blade (30%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Pull saw or push saw?

Western saws cut on the push stroke and use a thick blade so it doesn't buckle; they're quick in framing lumber. Japanese pull saws cut on the pull, so the blade can be thin: a narrower kerf, less effort and cleaner cuts. Pick a push saw for 2x lumber and a pull saw for joinery and trim.

What TPI do I need?

Fewer teeth per inch cut faster and rougher: 7 to 8 TPI for framing and yard work, about 11 TPI for general crosscuts in boards and plywood, 15 TPI and up for hardwood, trim and joinery.

What is a Ryoba?

A double-edge Japanese pull saw with rip teeth on one edge (for cutting along the grain) and crosscut teeth on the other, so one blade covers both kinds of cut.

What is a flush-cut saw for?

Trimming dowels, plugs and edging level with a surface. Its teeth have no set (they don't bend outward), so the blade can lie flat on the work without scratching it.

Can I sharpen a hand saw?

Most inexpensive saws have hardpoint (induction-hardened) teeth that stay sharp a long time but can't be filed. When they dull, you replace the saw, or just the blade on saws like the JORGENSEN 10-inch and the SUIZAN Ryoba.

Do I need a coping saw too?

Only if you cut curves or cope trim joints. Its thin, tensioned blade turns corners no straight saw can follow.
