How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from converts to seat (25%), capacity 330 (25%), big casters (25%), tool trays (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Plastic or padded?

One-piece plastic creepers are light, low and easy to clean; oil doesn't soak in. Padded steel creepers are more comfortable for long jobs but sit higher and the vinyl can tear.

Why do caster size and floor matter?

Bigger casters (3 inches) roll over cracks, cords and expansion joints. Small casters sit lower but catch on everything. On a rough or gravel surface, nothing rolls well.

Do I want a Z creeper?

Z creepers fold into a rolling seat, handy for brakes and wheels and for small garages. They're a little heavier and higher than a flat creeper.

How much ground clearance do I need?

The lower the creeper, the more room between you and the car. Low-profile creepers like the Lisle sit under an inch off the floor; padded creepers sit a few inches up.

Is it safe under a jack?

Never work under a car held only by a jack. Use jack stands on solid ground and chock the wheels.
