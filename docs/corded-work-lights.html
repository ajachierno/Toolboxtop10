How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $50; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from wet rated (30%), wattage listed (30%), dimmable (20%), tripod (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How many lumens do I need?

5,000-10,000 real lumens light a garage bay or room well. Budget lights often claim 20,000-30,000 lumens without a test standard; a stated wattage keeps them honest, since good LEDs produce roughly 100-150 lumens per watt.

Tripod or hanging?

Tripod lights aim where you're working and move with you. Hanging caged lights light a whole room evenly from above, which is better for drywall, painting and framing.

Do I need a weather rating?

For outdoor or wet sites, yes. Look for IP65/IP66 or an ETL or UL wet-location listing. Indoor-only lights can fail or shock in the rain.

What color temperature is best?

5000-6500K daylight white shows colors and defects clearly, which is what you want for paint and finish work. Warmer 3000K is easier on the eyes for long sessions.

Do LED work lights get hot?

Far less than halogen, which is why they replaced them. Aluminum housings still get warm, so keep them away from plastic sheeting and flammable materials.
