How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $25; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from rated hard (30%), adjustable jaws (25%), cushion grip (25%), a multi-piece set (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What size do I need?

8- to 14-inch cutters handle wire, small chain and bolts up to about 1/4 inch. 24- to 36-inch cutters give the leverage for 3/8- to 1/2-inch rod and heavy chain.

Hard vs soft ratings

Capacities are given by material hardness. Soft steel (under HRC 25) cuts easily; hardened steel (HRC 40+) like padlock shackles and high-grade chain needs a bigger cutter. Exceeding the rating chips the jaws.

Can it cut a padlock?

Small cutters usually can't cut hardened shackles. Use a 24-inch or larger cutter rated for hard metal, or an angle grinder.

Compact bolt cutters

Plier-size cutters like the KNIPEX CoBolt use compound leverage to cut bolts and hard wire with one hand. They're great in a tool pouch but won't handle chain.

Why adjustable jaws?

As blades wear, adjustment bolts or a cam bring them back together so they cut cleanly instead of crushing.
