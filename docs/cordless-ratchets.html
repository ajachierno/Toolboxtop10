How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $70; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a complete kit (30%), high torque (30%), a brushless motor (25%), extended (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How much torque does a cordless ratchet need?

Less than you'd think. Cordless ratchets are for running fasteners in and out quickly, not breaking them loose. 35-40 ft-lb handles most engine bay work; 60-75 ft-lb helps with stubborn bolts. Break tight bolts loose with a hand ratchet first to save the gears.

12V or 20V?

12V ratchets are smaller and lighter, which matters in tight engine bays. 20V ratchets have more torque but bigger bodies. If you already own a battery platform, stay on it.

Kit or bare tool?

If you own Milwaukee M12 or DEWALT 20V batteries, buy the bare tool. If you don't, a kit with two batteries and a charger is the cheapest way in, but those batteries won't run other brands' tools.

Do I need an extended head?

An extended neck reaches bolts tucked behind hoses, brackets and other parts. It's longer overall, so a standard head is better in cramped spaces with nothing in the way.

Can I use a cordless ratchet to torque bolts to spec?

No. Run the bolt down with the ratchet, then finish with a torque wrench.
