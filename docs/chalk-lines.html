How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $12 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from fast gear (35%), long line (25%), chalk included (20%), metal case (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

How long a chalk line do I need?

100 ft covers framing, roofing and most floors. A 30 to 50 ft compact reel is easier to carry for drywall, tile and cabinet layout. 150 ft only pays off on slabs and big decks.

What does the gear ratio mean?

It's how many times the spool turns for each turn of the crank. A 6:1 reel rewinds 100 ft in a few cranks; a reel with no gearing (1:1) takes many more. If you snap lines all day, a 4:1 to 6:1 gear saves real time.

Which chalk color should I use?

Blue is the standard for indoor work because it brushes and washes off. Red and other bold colors are more permanent and are meant for outdoor work where the line has to survive weather. Don't use red on finished surfaces.

Thin line or bold line?

A thin line (around 0.5 mm) leaves a precise mark for finish work and tile. A bold line (1 mm and up) carries more chalk and shows up on rough lumber, sheathing and concrete.

Does a chalk line work as a plumb bob?

Many do. Several reels here, including the IRWIN Classic, are shaped so the line hangs straight from the center of the case. A dedicated plumb bob is still more precise for tall drops.
