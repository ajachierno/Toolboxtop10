How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $90; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a complete kit (30%), brand battery (30%), a brushless motor (20%), long hose (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Can a cordless pressure washer replace a plug-in one?

Only the big ones. Handheld battery units are power cleaners for cars, bikes, boats, windows and patio furniture. For concrete, decks and siding you want 2,000 PSI or more, which on battery means a cart unit like the EGO HPW3204 at $879. If you have an outlet, a corded washer does that work for much less.

Can I trust the PSI on budget handheld washers?

Treat it as a ceiling, not a rating. Pressure and flow together take power: 1,500 PSI at 3 GPM needs roughly 2 horsepower of pump output, and a 21V handheld motor makes a fraction of that. WORX and EGO publish numbers that hold up. For unbranded units, compare reviews and runtime instead of the headline PSI.

Do I need a faucet?

No. Most of these draw water through a hose dropped into a bucket, pool or lake, and the WORX Hydroshot also connects to a 2-liter bottle. A weighted intake filter, like the one MIMYTH includes, keeps sand and leaves out of the pump. The Sun Joe WA24C-LTE carries its own 5-gallon supply.

Kit or bare tool?

Buy the bare tool only if you already own that battery platform: the WORX WG620.9 for Power Share owners or the ZixDeNex body for Milwaukee M18 owners. Otherwise a kit is cheaper than buying a tool, battery and charger separately.

How long does a battery last?

Listings claim 20 to 60 minutes per charge depending on battery size and pressure setting. Two-battery kits like the KEEPEAK, SEESII and AOODIL let you swap and keep going, which matters if you wash more than one car.
