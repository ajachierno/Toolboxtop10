The 10 Best Corded Impact Wrenches

Wired impact wrenches ranked from live Amazon data — the corded impact tool for lug nuts and bolts.

Quick note on names: there is no such thing as a corded impact driver — the impact-driver format (1/4-inch hex, for screws) is inherently cordless. The corded impact tool that actually exists is the impact wrench: a 1/2-inch square drive that spins sockets to break loose and tighten lug nuts, suspension bolts, and rusted hardware. This list covers electric corded impact wrenches — the ones that plug into a wall outlet (120V) or a car's 12V socket. If you want to drive screws, you want an impact driver from the cordless list; if you want to wrench on a car without an air compressor, you're in the right place.

Buyer's guide

Impact driver or impact wrench?

An impact driver has a 1/4-inch hex chuck for driving screws and small fasteners — and it's a cordless-only format. An impact wrench has a 1/2-inch (or 3/8") square drive that spins sockets to loosen and tighten bolts and lug nuts. If you want to drive screws, see the cordless impact drivers.

Corded (AC), 12V car, or air?

AC corded wrenches plug into a wall outlet for shop-grade power. 12V wrenches run off a car socket for roadside tire changes — handy but light-duty. Air (pneumatic) wrenches are powerful but need an air compressor. This list focuses on the electric ones you can plug in.

Why corded over cordless?

A corded electric impact wrench gives sustained power for less money than a comparable cordless model, and never runs out of charge — good for a home garage with an outlet. Cordless wins only if you need to work far from power.

How much torque do I need?

For car lug nuts (usually 80-100 ft-lbs to spec, more when rusted or over-torqued), aim for a wrench rated around 300-450 ft-lbs of breakaway so it actually cracks them loose. The 12V roadside tools sit well below that — fine for a spare, not for stuck bolts.

What's a hog-ring anvil?

It's a socket retainer that lets you push sockets on and pull them off by hand — fast and convenient. A detent-pin anvil holds sockets more securely but is slower to change. For general use, a hog ring is the friendlier choice.

Ingersoll Rand 231C 1/2" Air Impact Wrench

To be clear, this is a superb impact wrench and a shop standard. The catch: it's pneumatic. It doesn't plug into a wall outlet; it needs a good-sized air compressor to run. Buy it only if you already have one.

Why we'd skip it

  • It runs on compressed air, not electricity. There's no plug — you need an air compressor (realistically a $200+ pancake or larger) and a hose to use it at all.
