Buyer's guide

Will a manual tire changer handle my car's wheels?

Check your rim size first. The classic manual stands here (TUFFIOM, VEVOR, Olympia) stop at 16 in., and EASYBERG and MOTOOS at 16.5 in. Most cars sold in the last decade ride on 17 in. or larger wheels, which need a changer rated to 21 in. or more, like the LCTX or a full shop machine.

Do I need an air compressor?

Manual stands don't. The LCTX and the KATOOL shop machines use compressed air for the bead breaker or clamps, and the KATOOL ME-T500 and KT-T810 want 116 to 145 psi. A small pancake compressor won't keep up; plan on a tank of 20 gallons or more.

What is a bead blaster?

A bead blaster is a burst of air through a ring or nozzle that snaps a tire's bead onto the rim. Stiff run-flats, low-profile tires, and big off-road tires often won't seat with an ordinary air chuck. The KATOOL KT-T810 has one built in; for the others you can buy a separate bead seater tank.

Will a tire changer scratch alloy wheels?

A bare steel bar can. Look for a plastic or covered mount head and jaw covers. The KATOOL ME-T500 and KT-T810 include non-marring covers, and the Rabaconda uses a plastic mount head. With a manual stand, use rim protectors and plenty of tire lube.

Can I balance tires with a tire changer?

No. A changer only mounts and dismounts tires. You'll still need to balance the wheel afterward, either at a shop or with a wheel balancer. See our wheel balancer rankings for bubble and computerized options.
