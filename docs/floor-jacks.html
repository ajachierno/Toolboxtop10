How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from dual piston (25%), low profile (20%), high lift (20%), ansi (20%), three ton (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What capacity floor jack do I need?

A floor jack lifts one end or corner, so it carries well under the vehicle's full weight. A 2-ton jack covers most cars; 3-ton gives margin for SUVs, minivans and pickups. Check the curb weight and pick a jack rated at least three-quarters of it.

Low-profile or high-lift?

Measure from the ground to your car's jack point. Low-profile jacks start around 3 to 3.5 inches for sports cars and lowered sedans. SUVs and trucks need a jack that lifts 18 inches or more so you can fit a stand under them.

What does dual piston mean?

A dual-piston pump moves more fluid per stroke, so the saddle rises to the car in a few pumps instead of a dozen. It doesn't add capacity, just speed.

Can I work under a car on a floor jack?

No. Hydraulic jacks can leak down or slip. Lift the car, set jack stands under the manufacturer's support points, lower the car onto the stands, and only then get under it.

Aluminum or steel?

Aluminum jacks weigh less to carry around but cost more. Steel jacks are heavier and cheaper and do the same job in a home garage.
