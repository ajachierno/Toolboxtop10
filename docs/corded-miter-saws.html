Buyer's guide

10-inch or 12-inch miter saw?

A 12-inch blade cuts wider and taller stock on a fixed saw: the 12-inch DEWALT DWS715 crosscuts a 2x8, while the 10-inch DWS713 stops at a 2x6. A 10-inch saw is lighter, spins faster, and its blades cost less. If you cut mostly trim and 2x4s, 10 inches is enough.

Do I need a sliding miter saw?

Only if you crosscut boards wider than a fixed saw handles. Sliders like the SKIL MS6305-00 and DEWALT DWS780 cut a 2x12 or 2x14 in one pass. The catch is weight and depth: the rails stick out the back, so a slider needs clear space behind it. Bosch's glide arm on the CM10GD cuts that depth by up to 10 inches.

Single bevel or dual bevel?

A single-bevel saw tilts one way, so for a bevel in the other direction you flip the board end for end. That's fine for framing and occasional trim. Dual bevel tilts both ways and saves real time on crown molding and long runs of matched bevels.

Laser or LED shadow line?

A laser draws a line beside the blade and has to be calibrated, and it can drift. An LED shadow line (DEWALT XPS, SKIL) lights the blade from above so its shadow falls exactly where the teeth will cut, with nothing to adjust. If a saw has neither, a pencil mark and a slow first touch work fine.

Can I cut metal with a miter saw?

Not with a standard wood blade. Cutting steel needs a saw geared for it and a blade rated for metal. A wood-cutting saw can handle aluminum trim and PVC with the right non-ferrous blade, but don't take a listing's "cuts metal" claim at face value.

What should I upgrade first?

The blade. Most saws ship with a 24- to 40-tooth general-purpose blade. A 60- to 80-tooth finish blade makes a bigger difference to trim cuts than any feature on this list.

Why we'd skip it

  • The title says "Power Saw for Metal", yet it ships with a 36-tooth blade running at 5,000 rpm. Saws that actually cut metal slow down for it (the DOVAMAN here drops to 3,200 rpm) and use a blade made for metal. The bullets quietly narrow the claim to "soft metal".
  • The title says single bevel 0-45°. A bullet says the base adjusts "on both sides" for bevels, and the same bullet says miters go left only. The brand's own comparison chart says the bevel is left only. You can't tell from the listing what the saw does.
  • The spec table lists power as "1900 watt-hours" (a unit of stored energy, not motor power), blade material as alloy steel against a TCT blade in the title, and included components as "Bare Tool" while the bullets list a blade and two extension tables.
  • The brand, MarvTool, isn't in the title or the seller name (XXYD Official Direct), which makes parts and warranty support harder to track down.
