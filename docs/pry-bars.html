How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $15 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a multi-piece set (25%), strike cap (25%), a lifetime warranty (25%), made in usa (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

What size pry bar do I need?

Match the length to the force and the space. A 7 to 10 inch flat bar handles trim, molding and small nails. A 12 to 18 inch bar covers most general prying. A 24 to 36 inch bar or wrecking bar is for demolition and lifting heavy things, where leverage matters more than reach into tight spots.

Flat bar, handled bar or wrecking bar?

Flat bars are thin and slip into gaps, so they suit trim, flooring and nail pulling. Handled bars have a grip and usually a strike cap, so you can hammer the tip into place and pry hard without hurting your hands. Gooseneck wrecking bars are long, heavy demolition tools with a hook for spikes.

What does an indexing pry bar do?

Its head pivots and locks at several angles, so you can pry around an obstruction or from the side. Mechanics use them on suspension, exhaust and engine parts. The GEARWRENCH bars on this page lock in up to 14 positions.

Is a strike cap worth having?

Yes, if you'll drive the tip in with a hammer. A steel cap takes the blow instead of the plastic grip. On the best designs, like the Mayhew set here, the cap sits directly on the steel shaft.

Can I use a screwdriver as a pry bar?

You can, but you'll likely bend the shaft or chip the tip, and a slipping screwdriver is an easy way to cut yourself. A $13 to $15 flat bar is made for the job.
