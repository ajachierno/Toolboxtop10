How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from high speed (30%), platform (25%), big kit (25%), a brushless motor (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

12V or a pen-size 4V tool?

12V tools (Dremel 8240 and 8250, Milwaukee M12) can cut bolts, grind and drill like a corded Dremel. 4V pen-size tools are lighter and quieter for crafts, engraving, nail grinding and 3D-print cleanup, but their makers say they're not for cutting or heavy work.

Will Dremel accessories fit other brands?

Most rotary accessories use a 1/8-inch shank, and any tool with a 1/8-inch collet or a universal chuck takes them. Some pen tools, like the Fanttik F2 Master, only take 3/32-inch shanks. Dremel's own attachments (guides, flex shafts) fit Dremel tools.

Is a brushless rotary tool worth it?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer and get more out of each charge. On a 12V tool you use for cutting and grinding, it's a real gain. For light crafts it's not needed.

How long does the battery last?

It depends on speed and load. Pen tools run 20 minutes to a few hours on light work. 12V tools with removable batteries let you swap packs and keep going.

What safety gear do I need?

Eye protection every time; cut-off wheels can shatter. Wear a dust mask when sanding resin or 3D prints.
