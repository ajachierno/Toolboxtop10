Buyer's guide

How much torque do I need to take off car lug nuts?

Most passenger-car lug nuts are torqued to roughly 80-100 ft-lbs, so a 300-400 ft-lb wrench breaks them loose easily. Rusted nuts, trucks, and hardware someone overtightened with an air gun are where 1,000+ ft-lbs of breakaway torque earns its price.

What's the difference between fastening torque and breakaway torque?

Fastening torque is how hard the wrench tightens. Breakaway (or nut-busting) torque is how hard it can hit to loosen something stuck, and it is always the bigger number. When a budget listing shows one figure, check which one it is before comparing it to a DEWALT or Milwaukee spec.

Impact wrench or impact driver?

An impact wrench has a square drive (usually 1/2 inch) for sockets and is built for nuts and bolts. An impact driver has a 1/4-inch hex chuck for screw bits and is built for screws and lag bolts. If your job is wheels and car parts, get the wrench.

Kit or bare tool?

If you already own DEWALT 20V or Milwaukee M18 batteries, the bare tool saves $100 or more. If you don't, a kit is cheaper than buying the tool, a battery, and a charger separately. The budget brands only run on their own batteries, so buying one means starting a new battery platform.

What is a hog ring or friction ring anvil?

It is a spring ring around the square drive that holds a socket on by friction. Sockets snap on and off by hand, with no pin to line up. The alternative, a detent pin, holds sockets more securely but is slower to change.

1/2-inch or 3/8-inch drive?

1/2 inch is the standard for lug nuts and suspension work, and most of this list uses it. 3/8-inch tools are smaller and fit into engine bays and tight spaces, but they have less torque and need an adapter for 1/2-inch sockets.
