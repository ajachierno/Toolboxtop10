How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $35; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from lift 6 (30%), gentle incline (20%), rubber feet (20%), a stackable design (15%), pair (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How much capacity do I need?

Check your vehicle's curb weight. Ramps hold one end, so a pair rated at your curb weight or more covers it. Ratings are given per ramp, per pair or as GVW; read which one the number means.

How much lift?

6 to 7 inches is enough for an oil change on most cars. Trucks and SUVs benefit from 7 inches or more.

My car scrapes on ramps

Look for a gentle incline (17 degrees or less) or long ramps like the MaxxHaul 50709. Very low cars can use short jack-assist ramps to get a floor jack under them.

Are plastic ramps safe?

Good polypropylene honeycomb ramps are rated for heavy loads and won't rust. Use them on flat, hard ground, set the parking brake and chock the rear wheels.

Ramps or jack stands?

Ramps are faster for oil changes. Jack stands are needed when the wheels must come off or hang free.
