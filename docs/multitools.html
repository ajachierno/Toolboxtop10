How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from long warranty (30%), locking (25%), one hand (25%), sheath (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Which multi-tool should I start with?

For most people, a full-size pliers tool like the Leatherman Wingman or Wave+ or the Gerber Suspension NXT. If you only want something on your keys, the Gerber Dime. If you mostly need a knife, the Skeletool CX.

Do tool counts matter?

Less than you'd think. Many budget tools count each pliers function and each screwdriver size separately to reach 20+. Check the tools you'll actually use: pliers, a knife, flat and Phillips drivers, and scissors.

Why pay more for Leatherman?

Leatherman backs its tools with a 25-year warranty and makes the Wave+, Wingman and Skeletool in Portland, Oregon. The steel, fit and finish are better than budget tools.

Are locking tools important?

Yes. A lock stops a blade or screwdriver folding onto your fingers when you push. Most tools here lock; check before you buy.

Can I carry one on a plane?

Not in carry-on luggage in the US if it has a knife blade. Pack it in checked luggage.
