How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $80; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from amp 15 (30%), tool free tension (25%), auto oil (25%), bar 18 (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 9,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Corded or battery chainsaw?

A corded saw never runs out of charge and costs far less than a battery saw with a big battery. The trade is the cord: you need an outdoor extension cord and you stay within reach of an outlet. For cutting near the house or garage, corded is the cheaper choice. For the back of a large lot, see our cordless chainsaw page.

What bar length do I need?

A 16 in bar handles limbs and most firewood. An 18 in bar gets through bigger rounds in one pass but adds weight. Plan on cutting wood a couple of inches thinner than the bar length.

12 amp or 15 amp?

Amps are the most useful power number on a corded saw because every 120 V tool shares the same outlet limit. A 15 A saw holds its chain speed better in thick hardwood. A 12 A saw costs less and is fine for limbs and softwood.

What extension cord should I use?

Use an outdoor-rated cord. A thin or very long cord drops voltage and makes the motor work harder, so use the heaviest gauge your saw's manual calls for at the length you need.

What maintenance does a corded chainsaw need?

Keep the bar oil topped up, check chain tension before each use, and sharpen or replace the chain when it starts throwing dust instead of chips. The Oregon CS1500 sharpens its own chain.

Why don't we compare horsepower or watts?

Several listings print horsepower or 'peak watts' figures that a household outlet can't deliver, and they vary widely between similar saws. We show the amp rating, bar length and features instead.
