Buyer's guide

Bi-metal, carbide or carbon steel: which do I need?

Carbon steel saws are the cheapest and come in the biggest sizes, but they only cut wood, drywall and plastic. Bi-metal saws have high-speed steel teeth welded to a flexible body, so they cut wood and mild steel and last longer. Carbide-tipped saws are for stainless steel and other hard metal. If you'll ever drill a steel box or a metal door, buy bi-metal at minimum.

What size hole saw do I need for a door lock?

Most US door knobs and deadbolts need a 2-1/8 in hole through the face of the door and a 1 in hole in the edge for the latch. Check the lock's template first. DEWALT and others also sell dedicated lock installation kits with a jig that clamps to the door.

What is an arbor or mandrel?

The arbor (also called a mandrel) is the shaft that holds the hole saw and the pilot bit and goes into your drill's chuck. Small saws usually use one arbor and larger saws another. Quick-change arbors such as Diablo's Snap-Lock Plus, Milwaukee's quick-release and Bosch's SpinLOCK let you swap saws without unscrewing them.

Why does cutting depth matter?

A saw can only cut as deep as its cup. A 2x4 stud is 1-1/2 in thick, so a deep-cut saw like the Diablo (2-3/8 in) goes through a stud in one pass. Many cheap carbon steel kits cut only about 1 in deep, so thicker stock takes two passes from both sides.

How do I get the plug out of a hole saw?

Saws with wide slots let you push the plug out with a screwdriver. Some arbors, like Diablo's, eject the plug without tools. Drilling a small hole near the edge of the plug before you finish the cut also makes it easier to pop out.
