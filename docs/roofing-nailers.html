How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $110; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from depth adjust (30%), selectable trigger (20%), shingle guide (20%), dry fire lockout (15%), carbide tip (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What nails does a roofing nailer use?

Coil roofing nailers take 11-gauge, full-head, wire-collated 15-degree coils from 3/4 to 1-3/4 inches. Metabo HPT's model uses 16-degree coils.

Why a shingle guide?

The guide sets the exposure so each course lines up without measuring, which speeds up shingling and keeps rows straight.

Depth adjustment matters

Overdriven nails cut through shingles; underdriven nails stand proud and lift shingles. Tool-free depth adjustment lets you dial it in as conditions change.

Bump or sequential?

Bump (contact) firing is fast for production roofing. Sequential is safer and more precise.

Pneumatic or cordless?

Pneumatic roofers are lighter and faster for whole roofs. Cordless suits repairs where hauling a compressor up isn't worth it.
