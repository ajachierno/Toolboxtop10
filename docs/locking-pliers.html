How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from pieces 4 (30%), long nose (25%), wire cutter (25%), cushion grip (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What are locking pliers for?

They clamp onto a part and stay locked, freeing your hands. Use them to hold work for welding, grip rounded or stripped bolts, clamp parts while glue sets, or act as a temporary handle.

Which jaw shapes?

Curved jaws grip round stock and nuts. Straight jaws hold flat material. Long-nose jaws reach into tight spots. A C-clamp version holds pieces together for welding or gluing.

Why does the original Vise-Grip still sell?

IRWIN's trigger release and hardened teeth set the standard. Cheaper sets work fine for occasional use, but jaws and springs on budget sets can loosen sooner.

Trigger or lever-less release?

A trigger release gives more release force on tight clamps. Klein's lever-less design avoids pinching your palm.

Wire cutter worth it?

A cutter in the curved jaw handles soft wire in a pinch. It's handy, not a replacement for dedicated cutters.
