How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from variable speed (30%), auto tracking (20%), a vacuum port (20%), bench mount (15%), extra belts (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

3x21 or 4x24?

3x21 is the all-rounder: light enough to hold on doors and edges. A 4x24 removes material faster and stays flatter on wide surfaces like tabletops and floors, but it weighs 10 to 13 lbs. 3x18 sanders are lighter still and fine for small jobs.

What belt speed do I need?

Higher belt speed removes material faster. Variable speed lets you slow down for finishes, softwoods and paint that would otherwise gum up the belt. Look for a range listed in feet per minute.

What is auto-tracking?

Tracking keeps the belt centered on the rollers. Auto-tracking sanders do it on their own; others have a knob you adjust when you change belts.

Which grit should I start with?

40 or 60 grit to strip finishes or level uneven boards, 80 to 100 for general smoothing, 120 to 150 before finish sanding with an orbital sander.

Can I use a belt sander as a bench sander?

Some can be clamped upside down to a bench for shaping small parts. The VEVOR, Jellas and WEN HB424V include clamps for it.
