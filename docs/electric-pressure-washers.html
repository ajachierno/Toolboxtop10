How we rank

The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $150; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from certified (25%), onboard soap (25%), long hose (25%), premium motor (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 30,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Max PSI or rated PSI?

Max PSI is a brief peak at the nozzle; rated PSI is what the washer holds while you clean. Westinghouse and A-iPower list both; use the rated number to compare. Brands that are PWMA- or CETA-certified, or lab-tested like the Greenworks 3000, publish numbers that were checked independently.

How much PSI do I need?

1,500 to 2,000 PSI handles cars, bikes and patio furniture. 2,000 to 2,600 PSI is right for siding, fences and decks. For concrete driveways and stains, look at 3,000 PSI models like the Greenworks or Westinghouse WPX3400e.

Why does GPM matter?

GPM is how much water you move, and it decides how fast you rinse. Multiply PSI by GPM for 'cleaning units': a 2,000 PSI, 1.2 GPM washer makes 2,400; a 3,000 PSI, 2.0 GPM washer makes 6,000.

Induction, brushless or universal motor?

Most cheap electrics use a universal motor that's loud and wears out. Induction and brushless motors, like those in the Westinghouse WPX models and Greenworks 3000, run quieter and last longer.

Can I use an extension cord?

Manufacturers generally say to plug directly into a GFCI outlet and add hose length instead of cord length. A long, thin extension cord drops voltage and can overheat the motor.
