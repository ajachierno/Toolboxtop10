How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $95; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (35%), torque 300 (25%), battery included (20%), clutch (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What's a right-angle drill for?

Drilling and driving where a normal drill won't fit: between studs and joists, inside cabinets, under sinks and in engine bays.

Compact or HOLE HAWG?

Compact 3/8-inch angle drills drive screws and drill small holes in tight spots. HOLE HAWG-style 1/2-inch drills bore large holes through framing for wire and pipe with self-feed and auger bits.

Does torque matter?

For screws and small holes, 115 to 150 in-lb is plenty. For bigger bits and lag screws, look for 300 in-lb or more, or a HOLE HAWG.

Is an attachment good enough?

A right-angle attachment for your regular drill works for occasional use. A dedicated angle drill is shorter, lighter and much easier in tight spaces.

Brushless worth it?

Yes for regular use: brushless motors run cooler and get more done per charge.
