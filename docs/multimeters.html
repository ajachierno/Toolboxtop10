How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $15; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from cat rated (35%), trms (25%), auto range (20%), ncv (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 30,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What does the CAT rating mean?

CAT ratings say where a meter can safely be used: CAT II for plug-in appliances, CAT III for household wiring and panels, CAT IV for the service entrance. A CAT III-rated meter is built to withstand voltage spikes that can make an unrated meter fail violently. Unrated meters are fine for batteries and electronics, not mains.

Do I need True RMS?

True RMS reads accurately on distorted AC waveforms from dimmers, motors and electronics. For basic household voltage and car work, an average-responding meter is fine.

Auto-ranging or manual?

Auto-ranging picks the range for you, which is faster and easier for beginners. Manual ranging is cheaper and can be faster once you know what you're measuring.

What does non-contact voltage detection do?

NCV beeps and lights near a live wire without touching it, a quick safety check before you work on a circuit. Always confirm with a proper measurement too.

How do I test a car battery?

Set DC volts, red lead to positive, black to negative. About 12.6 V is fully charged with the engine off; 13.7-14.7 V with it running means the alternator is charging.
