The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $200; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

Buyer's guide

Steel drawers or a modular plastic system?

Steel chests and cabinets give you the most organized storage per dollar and hold heavy hand tools well. Modular systems like Milwaukee PACKOUT and DEWALT ToughSystem cost more per liter of storage but stack, lock together, and roll to the job. If the box lives in your garage, buy steel; if it travels, buy modular.

What do ball-bearing slides actually matter for?

Ball-bearing drawer slides open smoothly under load and pull out most of the way so you can reach the back. Cheap friction glides bind once a drawer is full of steel. Every cabinet you plan to load heavily should have ball-bearing slides — it's the single biggest quality tell.

How much load capacity do I need?

Per-drawer ratings (often 75–100 lb) matter more than the headline total. A drawer full of sockets and wrenches gets heavy fast. Look for at least 75 lb per drawer if you store metal hand tools, and don't trust a total-capacity number without a per-drawer figure behind it.

Do I need a lock?

If the cabinet lives in a shared shop, a rental, or a garage that others walk through, yes. A keyed central lock secures every drawer at once. Budget carts often skip it — factor that in if security matters.

What about the wheels?

Bigger casters roll better over cracks, cords, and gravel. Look for 5 in casters on steel cabinets and at least 7 in wheels on anything you'll pull across a driveway. Two locking casters keep the unit from drifting while you work.
