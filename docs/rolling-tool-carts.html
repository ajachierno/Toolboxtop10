How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from locking casters (25%), drawer (20%), rust proof (20%), tool holders (20%), handle storage (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 7,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Tool cart or tool chest?

A cart is open shelves you push to the job, so everything is in reach while you work. A chest is a stack of drawers that keeps tools organized and locked away. Many shops have both: a chest for storage and a cart for the job in front of them.

Plastic or steel?

Polypropylene and structural-foam carts don't rust, dent or chip, and they're quieter. Steel carts usually add a lockable drawer and pegboard sides, but the paint can chip and the steel can rust in a damp garage.

How much weight do I need?

Most service carts here are rated for 500 to 550 lbs total, which covers tools, parts and a small compressor. Check whether the rating is per shelf or total, and load the bottom shelf heaviest so the cart doesn't tip.

Do locking casters matter?

If you work on a sloped garage floor or lean on the cart, yes. A cart without brakes can roll away while you're working. Several carts here list braking casters; the WEN and MaxWorks don't.

What size casters should I get?

Five-inch casters roll over cords, cracks and expansion joints much more easily than small 3-inch wheels. Non-marring or rubber-wrapped wheels are quieter and won't scuff floors.
