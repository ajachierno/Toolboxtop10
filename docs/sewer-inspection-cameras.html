Buyer's guide

How long a cable do I need?

Measure from your cleanout to the street or septic tank and add 20 to 30 ft. For most houses 100 ft is enough. Long lots, rural properties, and commercial lines may need 165 ft. Only the full length is useful if the cable is stiff enough to push it there; 7 mm cables push farther than 5 mm.

What does self-leveling do?

A self-leveling head keeps the picture upright as the cable twists, so the bottom of the pipe stays at the bottom of the screen. It makes it much easier to judge sags, standing water, and which side a root is coming in from.

Do I need a locator?

A locator (512Hz sonde in the camera head plus a handheld receiver) tells you where the camera is under the yard and how deep. It's worth it if you plan to dig or need to mark a repair spot. If you just want to see whether there's a clog or roots, distance markings on the cable are enough.

Can I push a sewer camera through a toilet?

Not usually. Toilet traps are tight and can snag or crack the camera. Go in through a cleanout, or pull the toilet and enter through the flange. Clear standing clogs with a snake first; the camera can't get past a full blockage.

Will a $30 borescope work instead?

For a sink trap or a few feet of drain, maybe. For a sewer line, no: borescopes have 10 to 16 ft cables that are too floppy to push around bends, and they usually aren't rated for full submersion.
