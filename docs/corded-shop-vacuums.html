Buyer's guide

What does "peak HP" mean on a shop vac?

Peak horsepower is a motor rating measured with no load, and it isn't what you get while vacuuming. It's useful for comparing vacs from the same brand, but airflow (CFM) says more about suction. DEWALT lists 105 CFM for the DXV12P, for example, and Vacmaster lists 70 CFM for the VBVB611PF.

What tank size should I get?

2.5 to 4 gallons is enough for cars, stairs, and small spills, and those vacs are light enough to carry. 5 to 6 gallons covers most garage and workshop cleanup. Go to 12 to 16 gallons for flooded floors, renovation debris, or a woodshop, and pick one with a drain port so you don't have to lift a full tank of water.

Does hose diameter matter?

Yes. A 1-1/4 in. hose fits tight spots and car interiors but clogs on wood chips, leaves, and drywall chunks. A 1-7/8 in. or 2-1/2 in. hose moves more air and swallows bigger debris. If you do a lot of workshop cleanup, buy the wider hose.

Do I need to change the filter for wet pickup?

Usually. Most vacs use a paper or cloth cartridge for dry dust and a foam sleeve for water, and running water through a paper cartridge ruins it. Check which filters come in the box: the Vacmaster VOC508S and Amazon Basics both include a foam wet filter.

Poly tank or stainless steel?

Stainless tanks resist dents and cost a little more. Poly tanks are lighter, can't rust, and are what most of the bigger vacs use. Both hold water and debris the same way, so choose on size and features first.
