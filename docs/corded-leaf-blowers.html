Buyer's guide

CFM or MPH: which number matters?

CFM (cubic feet per minute) is how much air the blower moves, so it decides how big a pile you can push. MPH is how fast that air leaves the nozzle, which helps lift wet or stuck leaves. For lawns, compare CFM first. For hard surfaces and matted leaves, a higher MPH helps.

Should I get a 3-in-1 blower vacuum?

If you bag leaves, yes. A 3-in-1 blows leaves into a pile, then vacuums and shreds them so 10 to 18 bags of leaves fit in one. The trade-off is weight (8 to 12.5 lb here against 4 to 7 lb for blower-only models) and less airflow in blower mode on most of them.

What extension cord do I need?

None of these include one. Use an outdoor-rated cord, and go heavier the longer it gets: 16 gauge is fine up to about 50 ft for a 12-amp blower, and 14 or 12 gauge is the safer choice for 100 ft. Most of these blowers have a cord retainer to stop the plug pulling loose.

Corded or cordless?

Corded blowers cost less, weigh less for the same airflow and never stop for a battery. Cordless wins if your yard is far from an outlet or you'd rather not drag a cord around trees and beds. See our cordless leaf blowers page for battery models.

Is a backpack bag worth it?

Only on 3-in-1 models you use to vacuum a lot. A backpack bag moves the weight of collected leaves off the tool and onto your back, which is easier on your arm, but the whole setup is heavier: 11 to 12.5 lb on the two backpack picks here.
