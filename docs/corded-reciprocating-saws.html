The 10 Best Corded Reciprocating Saws

Wired demo saws ranked from live Amazon data — endless power for cutting, pruning, and tear-out.

A reciprocating saw (a.k.a. Sawzall) rips a long blade back and forth through wood, metal, PVC, nail-embedded lumber, and tree branches — the go-to for demolition and rough cuts. Corded models give you full, sustained power for the least money and never run flat, which matters when you're tearing out a wall or bucking a pile of branches. Amps are the number to watch: 7-8 amps handles most jobs, and 10-13 amps is heavy demolition. Want to cut anywhere with no cord? See the cordless list.

Buyer's guide

What's a reciprocating saw for?

It pushes a blade back and forth for fast, rough cuts through wood, metal, PVC, drywall, and nail-embedded lumber, plus pruning branches. It's the demolition and remodeling saw — not for fine straight cuts (that's a circular saw).

Why corded over cordless?

A corded saw gives full, sustained power for the least money and never runs out of charge — best for tear-outs, long cutting sessions, and a keep-forever demo saw. The trade is the cord and needing an outlet.

How many amps?

7-8 amps handles most home demolition and pruning. 10-13 amps is heavy-duty for tearing through framing, thick metal, and long jobs. More amps means more power and usually more weight.

Does stroke length matter?

Yes. A longer stroke (1-1/8 to 1-1/4 inch) uses more of the blade and cuts faster and more aggressively — better for demolition. Shorter strokes are smoother and easier one-handed.

Tool-free blade change and orbital action?

Tool-free blade change lets you swap blades without a wrench — get it; every pick here has it. Orbital action adds an oval blade motion for even faster wood cutting; it's a nice extra on higher-end saws but not essential.
