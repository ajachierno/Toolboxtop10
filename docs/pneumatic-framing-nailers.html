The list is ordered by that score. The features score is built from selectable trigger (30%), depth adjust (25%), rafter hook (25%), dry fire lockout (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 7,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

21°, 30° or 15° coil: which should I buy?

The angle decides which nails fit. 21° plastic strips with full round heads are the most common and are accepted by building codes everywhere. 30° paper strips fit tighter corners but often use clipped or offset heads, which some codes don't allow. 15° coils hold the most nails and are popular for sheathing and subfloor.

What size compressor does a framing nailer need?

Plan on 2 to 4 CFM at 90 PSI for steady framing. A 6-gallon pancake handles one nailer at a casual pace; for fast framing or two guns, use a bigger tank with 4+ CFM. See our air compressor list.

Do I need bump fire?

Bump (contact) mode fires each time the nose hits with the trigger held, which is fast for sheathing and subfloor. Sequential mode is safer and more precise for framing members. A selectable trigger lets you choose.

What's a rafter hook for?

It's a metal hook that lets you hang the nailer on a joist, rafter or ladder rung instead of setting it down. Pros working at height use it constantly.

Pneumatic or cordless framing nailer?

Pneumatic nailers are lighter, cheaper and drive nails all day, but you drag a hose and run a compressor. Cordless framers are heavier and more expensive, but there's no setup. For lots of framing, pneumatic wins on cost and weight.
