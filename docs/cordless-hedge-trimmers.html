How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from blade 22 plus (25%), battery included (25%), cut 3 4 (25%), dual action (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What blade length do I need?

18 to 20 inches for small shrubs and tight spots. 22 inches covers most hedges. 24 to 26 inches makes long, flat runs faster but adds weight.

What does cut capacity mean?

The thickest branch the blades can cut. 5/8 to 3/4 inch handles yearly trimming; overgrown hedges need 1 inch or more, like the EGO HT2601 or Greenworks HT2002.

Why dual-action blades?

Both blades move in opposite directions, which cuts faster and vibrates less than a single moving blade. Nearly every trimmer here is dual-action.

Hedge trimmer or pole hedge trimmer?

For hedges taller than you can comfortably reach, a pole hedge trimmer or an attachment on a pole saw saves using a ladder.

How do I keep the blades working?

Wipe off sap and spray a lubricant after each use. Sharp, clean blades cut cleaner and drain the battery less.
