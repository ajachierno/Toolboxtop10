How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from adjustable torque (30%), bit set (30%), an LED work light (20%), pivoting grip (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Cordless screwdriver or drill?

A cordless screwdriver is lighter, smaller and gentler: perfect for furniture, cover plates and hinges, where a drill can strip or snap small screws. For long wood screws, lag bolts or drilling holes, use a drill or impact driver.

Why do torque settings matter?

A clutch stops the driver when the screw is snug, so you don't strip the head or crack particleboard. That matters most on flat-pack furniture and electronics. Drivers without settings need you to stop the trigger yourself.

How much torque do I need?

Most 4V screwdrivers deliver 20 to 45 in-lb, enough for furniture and hardware. If you drive 2-inch wood screws, look at the 8V ENERTWIST (88 in-lb) or step up to a drill.

Built-in battery or swappable?

Most here have a built-in battery that charges over USB, which is convenient but means waiting when it's flat. The Metabo HPT and Milwaukee kits come with two swappable batteries, so one can charge while you work.

Do I need a pivoting handle?

A pivoting or two-position handle lets you switch between a straight stick grip for reach and a pistol grip for push and control. It's most useful inside cabinets and behind appliances.
