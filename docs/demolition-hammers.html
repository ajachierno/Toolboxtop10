How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $110; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from listed impact (30%), vibration control (25%), bits included (25%), a carrying case (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Demolition hammer, jack hammer or rotary hammer?

A rotary hammer drills and chisels. A demolition (chipping) hammer only chisels; most here say 'hammer only, no drilling.' 'Jack hammer' on Amazon usually means a bigger electric breaker for slabs. Real pavement breakers weigh 60+ lb.

How much impact energy do I need?

About 10 to 20 J removes tile, thinset and mortar. 40 to 65 J breaks 4-inch concrete slabs and footings. More energy means more weight, so match the tool to the job.

Are the wattage claims real?

Not always. A standard 15 A, 120 V outlet supplies about 1,800 W, so listings claiming 2,200 or 3,500 W are quoting a peak, not continuous power. Amps and impact energy (joules or ft-lb) are more useful.

SDS-Max or hex shank?

SDS-Max is the standard for demolition hammers up to about 25 lb and bits are easy to find. Bigger breakers use 1-1/8 or 1-3/16 inch hex. Buy bits that match your tool exactly.

What safety gear do I need?

Eye protection, hearing protection, gloves and a dust mask or respirator: breaking concrete makes silica dust. Wet the surface or use a vacuum shroud where you can.
