How we rank

The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $260; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from variable speed (40%), cast iron (30%), indexing (30%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 700 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What do the two numbers mean?

The first is swing: the largest diameter you can turn (twice the distance from the spindle to the bed). The second is the distance between centers: the longest spindle you can turn. A 12 x 18 lathe turns 12-inch bowls and 18-inch spindles.

Why does low speed matter?

Large or unbalanced blanks need slow speeds. A common rule is diameter in inches x RPM between about 6,000 and 9,000, so a 12-inch bowl blank should start around 500 to 750 RPM. Lathes whose slowest speed is 800+ RPM are for spindles and small work.

Variable speed or belt steps?

Electronic variable speed lets you dial speed up gradually as a blank comes into balance. Belt-step lathes are cheaper but you stop the lathe to move the belt. Many good lathes do both: belt ranges plus a dial.

Do I need reverse and indexing?

Reverse helps when sanding bowls. Indexing locks the spindle at set positions so you can lay out flutes or drill evenly spaced holes. Neither is essential for a first lathe.

What else do I need?

Turning tools (a spindle gouge, bowl gouge, skew and parting tool), a face shield, a sharpening setup and usually a 4-jaw chuck. Budget at least as much for accessories as for a cheap lathe.
