How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $100; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from bluetooth (30%), ac power (25%), charges batteries (25%), weather rated (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 4,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Do I need a radio that matches my tool batteries?

If you own cordless tools, yes. A radio on the same platform runs all day on packs you already charge, and most also take an AC cord for the shop. If you don't own tool batteries, a plug-in radio like the Sangean LB-100 is simpler.

What does 'charges batteries' mean?

Some radios, like the DEWALT DCR025 and Milwaukee's PACKOUT radio, charge your tool battery while plugged into the wall. That saves carrying a separate charger to the job.

Is Bluetooth range important?

Not much on a jobsite. 100 feet is plenty if your phone is in your pocket. Range drops through walls and around metal.

What does IP54 mean?

IP54 means protected against dust and splashing water from any direction. It's fine for light rain, not for leaving out in a storm.

How long will it run on a battery?

Small radios run 10 hours or more on a 2.0Ah pack. Makita claims up to 35 hours on a 5.0Ah pack for the XRM06B. Volume and bass settings make a big difference.
