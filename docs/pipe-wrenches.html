How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from aluminum (30%), capacity 2 (25%), a multi-piece set (25%), replaceable jaws (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Aluminum or cast iron?

Aluminum wrenches are about 40% lighter with the same jaws, which matters when you use them overhead or all day. Cast iron is cheaper and fine for occasional use.

What size do I need?

A 10- to 14-inch wrench handles most household pipe. An 18-inch reaches 2-1/2-inch pipe and gives more leverage. 24-inch and up is for large pipe and stubborn joints.

Why use two wrenches?

Use one wrench to hold the fitting and the other to turn the pipe, so you don't twist or crack the rest of the line.

Will it damage the pipe?

The teeth bite to grip, so they mark the surface. Wrap chrome or finished pipe with a rag, or use a strap wrench instead.

Replaceable jaws

On RIDGID and DURATECH-style wrenches, the hook jaw, heel jaw and spring can be replaced when the teeth wear, so the wrench lasts for decades.
