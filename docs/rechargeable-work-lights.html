How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $10; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from magnetic tips (30%), ip rated (25%), platform (25%), usb c (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 20,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How bright should a work light be?

300-1,000 lumens covers engine bays and cabinets. 2,000-7,000 lumens lights a garage or job area. Treat big numbers on small lights with suspicion; a stated wattage and battery size keep claims honest.

Magnetic or tool-battery?

Magnetic pocket lights stick to car bodies and steel and charge by USB. Tool-battery lights (DEWALT, Klein) run longer and survive jobsite drops, and you probably already own the battery.

What is FL1?

ANSI FL1 is a test standard for flashlight and work light output and runtime. An FL1 rating means the lumens and hours were measured the same way across brands.

Do I need a water-resistance rating?

For outdoor or wet work, yes. IP65 handles rain and splashes. Most pocket mechanic lights have no rating and shouldn't be left in the rain.

How long do they run?

Runtime drops sharply on high. Pocket lights run 2-4 hours on high; tool-battery and large-battery lights run much longer. Check runtime at the brightness you'll actually use.

  • It claims 120 W and 10,000 lumens from a 14-ounce, 6.5 x 4.3-inch light. The TRESDA flood light needs a 100 W system and a 10,800 mAh battery for 7,000 lumens.
