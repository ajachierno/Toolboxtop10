The list is ordered by that score. Buyer's guide

Iron, station or gun?

A pencil iron plugs straight into the wall and is fine for occasional repairs. A station puts the controls in a separate base, holds temperature better under load, and usually has a proper holder. A soldering gun is for heavy wire, lugs and stained glass, and is too hot for circuit boards.

How many watts do I need?

For electronics, 60-80 W with temperature control is plenty. More watts mostly means faster recovery when the tip touches a big joint. High wattage without temperature control is a liability on boards.

What temperature should I solder at?

Leaded 60/40 solder melts at about 370°F (188°C) and is usually worked around 600-650°F. Lead-free solder melts around 423°F (217°C) and usually needs 650-700°F. Go hotter only for large joints.

Does auto sleep matter?

Yes, if you leave the iron on between jobs. Dropping the temperature when idle slows oxidation and makes tips last much longer, and it's a safety net if you forget to switch off.

What does the Pinecil need to run?

A USB-C charger that supports PD or QC 3.0, or a DC barrel supply. None is in the box, and a weak phone charger won't give it full power.

Why do tip series matter?

Spare tips fit only their own series (900M, T18, T19 and so on). Check what's included and what replacement tips cost before you buy, because tips wear out.
