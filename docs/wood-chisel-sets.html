How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $15; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from six plus (30%), strike cap (25%), sharpening (25%), storage (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Which sizes do I need?

1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and 1 inch cover most work, from hinge mortises to cleaning out joints. Add 1-1/4 and 1-1/2 inch for wider paring.

Bevel-edge or mortise chisels?

Bevel-edge chisels like these are all-rounders for paring, trimming and light chopping. Mortise chisels are thicker for heavy chopping deep mortises.

What steel is best?

Chrome-vanadium and chrome-manganese steels hardened to about HRC 58-62 hold a good edge and still sharpen easily. Harder steel holds an edge longer but is more brittle.

Do I need to sharpen new chisels?

Usually yes. Factory edges are rarely as sharp as they should be. Flatten the back and hone the bevel before use; sets with a stone and honing guide make this easy.

Hammer or mallet?

Use a wooden or rubber mallet on wood handles. Chisels with a steel strike cap can take a hammer for site work.
