Buyer's guide

Telescoping or low profile?

Telescoping (high-lift) jacks start around 33 to 35 in. and reach 67 to 74 in. They're built for cars on a two-post lift. Low-profile jacks start at about 7 to 8 in. and top out around 23 to 27 in., so they're for cars on jack stands. Pick the one that matches how you lift the car.

How much capacity do I need?

Most car automatics weigh roughly 150 to 250 lb dry, and many light-truck automatics run 200 to 350 lb. A 450 lb jack covers most cars. For trucks, transfer cases, and a margin for torque converter fluid, a 1,000 lb or larger jack is the safer pick.

Can I use a floor jack instead?

Not safely on its own. A floor jack's saddle is small, it rises in an arc, and there's nothing to strap the transmission to. Bolt-on adapter plates exist, but they're usually rated around 300 lb. A real transmission jack has a wide saddle with tilt adjustment and chains.

Why does saddle tilt matter?

The transmission's input shaft has to slide straight into the engine's pilot bearing or torque converter. A saddle that tilts forward, back, and side to side lets you match the engine's angle instead of muscling the transmission into place.

Do I need a foot pedal?

It helps. With the car on a lift you'll have both hands on the transmission, so a foot pedal lets you raise it without letting go. The telescoping jacks here all use a foot pedal; the low-profile ones use a hand pump.
