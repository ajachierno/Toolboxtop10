How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from weatherproof (25%), boost (25%), warranty 2yr (20%), power bank (15%), display (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 50,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How many amps do I need?

Ignore the peak amp number: there's no standard for it, and budget brands inflate it. Use the maker's rated engine size instead. A 4-cylinder car needs a pack rated for about 4.0L gas; V8 trucks and diesels need one rated for their engine size or bigger.

Lithium or lead-acid?

Lithium packs weigh 1 to 7 lbs and double as power banks. Lead-acid (AGM) boxes like the Clore weigh 15-20 lbs but handle repeated jumps and cold weather well, which is why shops use them.

What does boost or force-start mode do?

Most packs won't send power unless they detect some voltage in the car's battery, as a safety feature. If the battery reads near zero, boost mode overrides that check. Use it carefully and only with the clamps on the right terminals.

How do I keep a jump starter ready?

Recharge it every 3 months, and after every use. Keep it out of extreme heat on the dashboard. A dead jump starter is just a paperweight.

Is a jump starter the same as a battery charger?

No. A jump starter gives a short burst to crank the engine; the car's alternator then charges the battery. If the battery keeps dying, test it or charge it with a proper charger.
