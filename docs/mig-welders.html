How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $115; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from gas mig (30%), dual voltage (25%), aluminum ready (25%), synergic (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Flux core or gas MIG?

Flux-core wire makes its own shielding, so there's no gas bottle and it works outdoors in wind; welds are spattery and need cleanup. Gas MIG uses solid wire and a CO2/argon bottle for clean welds on thinner metal, best indoors. Many welders here do both.

110V or 220V?

110V machines plug into a garage outlet and handle sheet metal up to about 3/16 inch. 220V roughly doubles the usable power for thicker steel. Dual-voltage welders give you both.

Can I weld aluminum?

Usually only with a spool gun (sold separately) and argon gas. Welders marked spool-gun ready, like the ARCCAPTAIN MIG200/MIG250 and YESWELDER MIG-205DS, can do it.

What is synergic control?

You pick the wire size or metal thickness and the welder sets matching voltage and wire speed. It makes learning much easier; experienced welders can switch to manual.

What else do I need?

An auto-darkening helmet, leather gloves and jacket, a wire brush and grinder, clamps, and for gas MIG a bottle of shielding gas. Weld in a ventilated area and keep a fire extinguisher close.
