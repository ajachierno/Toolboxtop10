How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from both SAE and metric sizes (30%), storage (25%), a lifetime warranty (25%), long pattern (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 6,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

SAE, metric or both?

Most cars, bikes, appliances and furniture built in the last 30 years use metric. Older American cars, trucks, trailers and farm gear use SAE. A set with both covers almost everything around a house.

12-point or 6-point?

A 12-point box end grabs a bolt at twice as many angles, handy in tight spots. A 6-point end holds more of the bolt's flats and is less likely to round off a stubborn or rusty bolt. Most sets here are 12-point; GEARWRENCH also sells 6-point sets.

What's long pattern?

Long-pattern wrenches have longer handles for more leverage on stuck bolts. They can be harder to swing in tight engine bays.

Do I need ratcheting wrenches?

Not to start. Plain combination wrenches are cheaper, stronger and thinner. Add a ratcheting set later for repetitive work in tight spaces.

What about warranty?

CRAFTSMAN lists a full lifetime warranty on the 32-piece set, and EFFICERE backs its 24-piece set with a lifetime guarantee. Hand tools rarely break, but a free replacement is nice if one does.
