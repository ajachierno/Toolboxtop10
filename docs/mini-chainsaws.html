How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $40; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from two batteries (25%), auto oil (25%), a brushless motor (25%), bar 8 (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 20,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What's a mini chainsaw good for?

Pruning shrubs and small trees, cutting branches up to about 4 to 6 inches, and storm cleanup of small limbs. They're not for felling trees or cutting firewood logs.

6-inch or 8-inch?

6-inch saws are lighter and easier one-handed. 8-inch bars cut thicker limbs. Kits with both bars give you the choice.

Are the wattage claims real?

Treat them as marketing. A 21V 2Ah battery holds about 42 watt-hours, so a mini saw can't run at 1,000 W for long. Look at cut counts, battery size and reviews instead.

Do I need to oil it?

Yes. Auto-oilers feed bar oil while you cut; others need oil added by hand. Running a dry chain ruins it quickly.

Are they safe?

They're easier to control than a full saw, but they still cut flesh. Use both hands when you can, wear gloves and eye protection, and keep the guard on.
