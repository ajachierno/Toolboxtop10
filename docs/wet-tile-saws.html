How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $105; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a sliding rail for wider cuts (30%), large tile (30%), water control (20%), a stand (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 7,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

7-inch tabletop or 10-inch sliding saw?

7-inch tabletop saws cut 12-inch tile and smaller and suit backsplashes and bathrooms. 10-inch sliding saws carry the tile on a cart past the blade, which gives straighter cuts on large-format porcelain and planks.

Do I need a handheld wet saw?

Handheld wet saws cut tile that's already installed, notches, countertops and stone. Most tilers use one alongside a tabletop or sliding saw.

Which blade do I need?

Use a continuous-rim diamond blade for clean porcelain and ceramic cuts. Porcelain is hard and brittle, so a porcelain-specific blade chips less.

How do I reduce chipping?

Feed slowly, keep the water flowing, support the whole tile and cut with the glazed face up. A fresh, sharp blade helps most.

Can I use a wet saw indoors?

Yes, with a splash guard or containment like SKIL's HydroLock and a drop cloth. Wet cutting keeps silica dust down, which matters for your lungs.
