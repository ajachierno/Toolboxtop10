How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from metric sae (30%), a ratcheting handle (25%), pitch gauge (25%), wide range (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What's the difference between a tap and a die?

A tap cuts internal threads in a hole. A die cuts external threads on a rod or bolt. Most repair work is chasing damaged threads rather than cutting new ones.

Taper, plug or bottoming tap?

A taper tap starts threads easily. A plug tap goes deeper. A bottoming tap finishes threads to the bottom of a blind hole. Sets with both taper and plug taps handle more jobs.

Bearing steel or HSS?

Most affordable sets use GCr15 bearing steel, fine for chasing threads and soft metals. High-speed steel lasts longer and cuts hard steel and stainless better.

Do I need a pitch gauge?

Yes, if you don't know the thread. A pitch gauge tells you threads per inch or millimeter pitch so you pick the right tap.

Tips for clean threads

Drill the correct tap-drill size, use cutting oil, and back the tap off a half turn every turn or two to break chips.
