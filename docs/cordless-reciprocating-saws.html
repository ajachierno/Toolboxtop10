How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 50% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 15% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a complete kit (30%), a brushless motor (25%), variable speed (15%), tool-free adjustments (15%), orbital action (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 60,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What's a reciprocating saw for?

It pushes a blade back and forth to make fast, rough cuts through almost anything — wood, metal, PVC, drywall, and nail-embedded lumber — plus pruning branches. It's the demolition and remodeling saw; not for fine, straight cuts (that's a circular saw).

Bare tool or kit?

A bare tool ships with no battery or charger — great if you own the brand's packs, useless out of the box if you don't. A kit includes a battery, charger, and often blades. Each pick above says which.

Does stroke length matter?

Yes. A longer stroke (around 1-1/8 to 1-1/4 inch) uses more of the blade and cuts faster and more aggressively — better for demolition. Shorter strokes (7/8 inch) are gentler and easier one-handed, fine for pruning and light cuts.

Brushless or brushed?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and hold power under load — worth it for heavy demolition. For occasional pruning and the odd cut, a brushed saw like the BLACK+DECKER BDCR20B is plenty.

Which battery platform?

Buy on the same platform as tools you already own (DEWALT 20V MAX, Milwaukee M18, RYOBI ONE+, CRAFTSMAN V20). If you own nothing yet, a self-contained kit like the SEESII avoids the platform lock-in.
