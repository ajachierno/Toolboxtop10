Buyer's guide

Will an OBD2 scanner work on my car?

Every car and light truck sold in the U.S. since 1996 has an OBD2 port, usually under the dashboard near the steering column. Basic engine codes work on all of them; ABS, airbag and other system coverage varies by brand and year.

Code reader or scan tool?

A code reader reads and clears check engine codes. A scan tool adds live sensor data, and the better ones read ABS, airbag, transmission and TPMS codes. If you've had a brake or airbag light, you need a scan tool.

Handheld or Bluetooth?

Handhelds plug in and work with no phone or account. Bluetooth adapters are smaller and use your phone's screen and an app, which can add repair reports, logging and vehicle-specific features.

Should I clear the check engine light?

Only after fixing the problem. Clearing the code turns off the light, but it also resets the emissions monitors, and the car may fail an inspection until they're ready again.

What does bidirectional mean?

A bidirectional tool can send commands to the car, such as turning on the fuel pump, cycling ABS valves or retracting an electronic parking brake. It's useful for testing parts, not just reading what's wrong.
