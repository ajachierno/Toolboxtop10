How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $70; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from speed control (30%), a complete kit (25%), anti drip (25%), an LED work light (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Is a powered caulk gun worth it?

If you caulk more than a few tubes at a time, yes. A motor pushes the plunger at a steady rate, so beads are more even and your hand doesn't cramp on thick adhesives or cold silicone.

What does anti-drip do?

When you release the trigger, the plunger backs off slightly so caulk stops flowing instead of oozing out of the nozzle. It saves mess on finished surfaces.

Do I need speed control?

Yes for mixed jobs. Slow speeds give a thin bead on trim; fast speeds move thick construction adhesive. Single-speed guns like the AA-powered MARSOSAUR work best on standard 10 oz tubes of caulk.

Will it take sausage packs or 29 oz tubes?

Most guns here take standard 10 oz cartridges only. DEWALT's DCE560B has interchangeable trays for other canister sizes.

Kit or bare tool?

If you own DEWALT, Milwaukee, CRAFTSMAN or RYOBI batteries, the bare tool keeps you on one platform. If not, the SEESII and Uaoaii kits include two batteries and a charger for less than most bare brand-name guns.
