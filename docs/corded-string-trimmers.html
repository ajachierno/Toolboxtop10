How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from strong motor (30%), auto feed (25%), wide swath (25%), edger (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 16,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Corded or cordless string trimmer?

Corded trimmers cost less, weigh less and run as long as you like, but you drag a cord and stay within reach of an outlet. They suit small and medium yards. If your fence line is 150 feet from the house, a battery trimmer is easier.

How many amps do I need?

For a normal lawn, 5 to 6.5 amps handles grass and ordinary weeds. The 3 to 4 amp models are fine for edging and touch-ups after mowing but slow down in thick growth.

What swath width should I get?

The swath is the diameter the line cuts. 13 to 15 inches covers ground faster on a full yard; 10 to 12 inches is lighter and easier to steer around beds and posts.

Auto feed, push-button or bump feed?

Auto feed lets out line on its own, push-button feeds when you press a button, and bump feed needs you to tap the spinning head on the ground. Auto and push-button are easier for most people.

What extension cord should I use?

None of these include one. Use an outdoor-rated cord: 16-gauge is fine up to about 50 feet, and 14- or 12-gauge is the safer choice at 100 feet so the motor gets full power. Most of these trimmers have a cord-retention hook so the plug doesn't pull apart.

What does edger mode do?

On a trimmer-edger the head rotates 90 degrees so the line cuts vertically along a sidewalk or bed. Models with a wheel or wire edge guide hold a straighter line than freehand edging.
