How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (35%), a 2-speed gearbox (20%), an LED work light (15%), two batteries in the box (15%), a complete kit (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 60,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Voltage: 12V or 20V?

12V drills are lighter and cheaper and handle furniture, shelves, and small jobs. 20V drills have the torque for drilling into hardwood, driving long screws, and general home projects. If you only own one drill, buy 20V.

Brushless or brushed?

Brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and squeeze more runtime from a battery. They cost more. For occasional DIY, a brushed drill like the DCD771 is fine; if you'll use it weekly, pay up for brushless.

How much torque do I need?

Torque is twisting power. Under ~300 in-lbs (or ~35 Nm) is light-duty — screws and small bits. Name-brand 20V drills push well past that for drilling into wood and metal. None of these are rated for concrete or masonry.

One battery or two?

Two batteries mean you never stop to charge. Kits like the DCD771 and PULITUO include a spare; most budget drills ship with one, so factor a second battery into the price.

What does chuck size mean?

The chuck holds the bit. A 3/8 in chuck fits most home bits; a 1/2 in chuck accepts larger bits and grips better for heavy drilling. Keyless chucks let you swap bits by hand.

Why we'd skip it

  • The spec sheet claims a 30,000 rpm max speed while the description says 1,500 rpm. A drill does not spin at 30,000 rpm — that is a rotary-tool number. When a listing contradicts itself on the headline spec, you can't trust anything else on the page.
  • "JH Steady" is a no-name brand with no service record, and the battery is a proprietary 21V pack. When it dies you are buying a whole new drill, not a replacement battery.
  • The page sells several different "kit options" under one listing, so its ratings may not even be for this exact drill — you can't tell what the stars are actually rating.
  • "21V" is marketing inflation. Serious drills run 18V or 20V MAX; padding the number to 21 is a spec game, not extra power.
