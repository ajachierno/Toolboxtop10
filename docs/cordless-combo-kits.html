How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $160; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (35%), two batteries (25%), half inch chuck (20%), bag or case (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 60,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Why buy a combo kit?

A drill and an impact driver cover nearly every drilling and driving job, and the kit price usually costs less than the two tools and batteries bought separately. More importantly, it puts you on a battery platform you'll use for future tools.

Which battery platform?

Pick the one whose other tools you'll want. DEWALT 20V MAX, Milwaukee M18 and Makita 18V LXT have the widest ranges. CRAFTSMAN V20, RYOBI ONE+ and Greenworks 24V are cheaper with fewer pro tools.

12V or 18V/20V?

12V tools are smaller and lighter, great for cabinets, electrical and overhead work. 18V/20V tools have more power for big holes, long screws and masonry.

Is brushless worth it?

Yes if you use the tools often: brushless motors run cooler, last longer and get more work per charge. For occasional home use, a brushed kit like the DCK240C2 is fine.

What does a hammer drill add?

A hammer mode pounds as it spins, for drilling small holes in brick and concrete. It's useful for anchors; for big masonry holes, use a rotary hammer.
