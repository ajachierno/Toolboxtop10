How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $12; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from quick change (30%), blade storage (25%), metal body (25%), spare blades (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Retractable, folding or fixed?

Retractable knives slide the blade back into the handle and are safest in a toolbox. Folding knives close like a pocket knife and ride on a belt clip. Flip-open knives like Milwaukee's Fastback and DEWALT's push-and-flip open with one hand.

What blades do they take?

Nearly all of these take the standard trapezoid utility blade sold everywhere. Buy blades in bulk; a sharp blade is safer because you push less.

Does blade storage matter?

It saves hunting for a spare on a ladder. The Klein 44306, REXBETI set and Milwaukee 48-22-1502 store blades in the handle.

Metal or plastic body?

Aluminum bodies like the WORKPRO and Amazon Basics folding knife survive drops and look better longer. Plastic knives are fine for a junk drawer.

Is a utility knife the same as a pocket knife?

No. A utility knife takes cheap replaceable blades you swap when dull. A pocket knife has a fixed blade you sharpen. For boxes, carpet and drywall, a utility knife is easier to keep sharp.
