Wired multi-tools ranked from live Amazon data — full power for flush cuts, plunge cuts, and scraping.

Ranked by ToolboxTop10 from Amazon data captured Nov 10, 2026. Rankings and prices are updated several times a week and can change as new data comes in. How we rank

An oscillating multi-tool vibrates a blade side to side thousands of times a minute to flush-cut trim, plunge-cut drywall, scrape caulk, and sand into corners. Corded models give you full, sustained power for the least money and never run out of charge — ideal for a workbench or long remodeling sessions. How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 50% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 15% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $45 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a complete kit (30%), variable speed (30%), tool-free adjustments (20%), extra accessories (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Why corded over cordless?

A corded multi-tool gives you full, constant power for the least money and never runs flat — best for a workbench or long remodeling jobs. The trade is the cord and a wall outlet. Want to work anywhere? See the cordless list.

How many amps do I need?

Around 3 amps and up cuts confidently through wood, nails, and grout. 2 to 2.5 amps is fine for trim, drywall, and light work. Below about 2 amps, the tool stalls on anything tough — avoid the sub-2-amp 'mini' models.

Do the blades fit any brand?

Look for a 'universal' tool-free interface that accepts standard OIS/universal blades — nearly all the picks here do. Avoid tools that only take proprietary blades; you'll pay a premium for accessories forever.

What does oscillation angle mean?

It's how far the blade swings each stroke. A wider angle (around 3-4.5°) cuts faster and more aggressively; a narrower angle is smoother and more controlled. Some tools let you trade speed for control.

Variable speed — worth it?

Yes. High speed cuts wood and metal fast; low speed keeps plastic and paint from melting and gives more control for detail work. Every pick above has it.
