How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $220 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (30%), selective (30%), a complete kit (20%), long warranty (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Cordless or pneumatic finish nailer?

Cordless nailers skip the compressor and hose, which saves setup time on small jobs and punch lists. They're heavier (6 to 9 lbs vs 4 lbs) and cost more. For all-day production trim, an air nailer is lighter and cheaper.

Angled or straight?

Angled magazines reach into corners and under cabinets more easily, and most 15-gauge nailers are angled. Straight 16-gauge nails cost less.

Do cordless nailers have a delay?

Some flywheel designs spin up for a moment before firing. Air-spring designs like Metabo HPT's fire immediately, closer to a pneumatic nailer.

How many nails per charge?

Makers quote 375 to 800 nails per charge on a 2.0Ah battery, depending on the tool and the wood. A spare battery covers a full day of trim.

Can a finish nailer do framing?

No. Finish nailers drive 15 or 16-gauge nails for trim. Framing, sheathing and subfloor need a framing nailer with 2- to 3-1/2-inch clipped or round-head nails.
