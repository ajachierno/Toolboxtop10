How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $20 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a ratcheting handle (35%), heat shrink (20%), interchangeable dies (15%), a built-in wire cutter (15%), color coded (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Ratcheting or plier crimper?

A plier crimper is cheap, slim and usually has a cutter, but how hard you squeeze decides the crimp. A ratcheting crimper won't open until it has completed the full stroke, so every crimp gets the same force. If you crimp more than a few terminals a year, the ratchet is worth it.

Do I need a special crimper for heat-shrink connectors?

Yes. Heat-shrink butt connectors have a thin sleeve that standard insulated-terminal dies can split. Use a crimper made for heat-shrink connectors, like the Plustool, the haisstronica HS-8327 or the heat-shrink die in a set. TITAN and Klein both say their insulated-terminal crimpers are not for heat shrink.

What do the red, blue and yellow colors mean?

They are the standard insulated terminal sizes: red for 22-16 AWG, blue for 16-14 AWG, yellow for 12-10 AWG. Color-coded cavities on the crimper match the terminal's color, so you can't pick the wrong die.

Can these crimp battery cable lugs?

No. Everything here tops out at 10 AWG. Battery and welding cable lugs (8 AWG up to 4/0) need a hammer crimper or a hydraulic crimper.

Do I still need a wire stripper?

Usually. Plier crimpers often strip small wire badly or not at all. The Klein 80013 kit includes an automatic stripper; otherwise see our wire strippers page.
