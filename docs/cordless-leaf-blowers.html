How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from high cfm (30%), battery included (25%), variable speed (25%), turbo (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 15,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

CFM or MPH: which matters?

CFM (cubic feet per minute) is how much air moves; MPH is how fast. CFM moves piles of leaves; MPH lifts wet, stuck debris. Look at both. A blower with high MPH and low CFM is fine for patios, not for a lawn full of leaves.

How much CFM do I need?

100 to 200 CFM clears patios, decks and garages. 400 to 600 CFM handles most suburban yards. 700 CFM and up competes with gas blowers for big properties and wet leaves.

What about the 'RPM' claims?

Budget listings advertise motors at 800,000 to 990,000 RPM. RPM tells you nothing about airflow. Skip blowers that don't list CFM and MPH.

Battery platform or standalone?

If you'll add a trimmer, mower or chainsaw, buy into one platform (EGO, Greenworks, DEWALT, BLACK+DECKER) so batteries swap between tools. Budget kits' batteries usually fit nothing else.

How long will the battery last?

On turbo, minutes, not hours. EGO lists up to 75 to 90 minutes at low speed. Use the lowest setting that works and save turbo for stubborn spots.
