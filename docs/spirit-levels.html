How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $10; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from magnetic tips (25%), aluminum (20%), angle vial (20%), long 24 (20%), accuracy listed (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What length do I need?

A 9- to 12-inch torpedo level handles pictures, shelves and appliances. 24 inches suits cabinets and windows. 48 inches is for framing, doors and decks, where a short level misses bows.

How accurate are levels?

Good spirit levels are accurate to about 0.5 to 1 mm per meter. Check a level by reading it, flipping it end for end on the same spot and reading again; the bubble should sit the same.

Magnetic or not?

Magnets hold the level on steel studs, pipe, conduit and machinery, leaving both hands free. They're no help on wood.

What's a pitch vial?

A pitch vial reads slope, typically 1/8 or 1/4 inch per foot, the range used for drain pipes and gutters.

Digital or bubble?

Digital and light-up levels are easier to read at odd angles and in the dark. Bubble levels never need batteries.
