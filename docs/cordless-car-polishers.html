How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from dual action (30%), a complete kit (25%), a brushless motor (25%), platform (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Is a cordless polisher as good as a corded one?

For waxing and light correction, yes, and there's no cord to drag across the paint. Heavy correction drains batteries fast, so pros often keep a corded polisher for that and a cordless one for touch-ups.

Dual action or rotary?

Dual-action polishers are forgiving and won't burn paint, ideal for beginners. Rotaries cut faster but can burn through clear coat if held still.

What does throw mean?

Throw is how far the pad orbits off-center. 5-8 mm throws are easy to control; 15 mm long-throw polishers like Milwaukee's correct faster on flat panels.

How long do the batteries last?

Budget kits run about 20-40 minutes per 2.0Ah battery, so two batteries cover a waxing session. Tool-platform batteries (4-6Ah) last longer.

Do I need special pads?

Use cutting pads with compound for swirls, polishing pads with polish to refine, and finishing pads for wax. Match pad size to the backing plate.
