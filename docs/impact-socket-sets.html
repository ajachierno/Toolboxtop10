How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from half inch (25%), deep shallow (25%), a carrying case (25%), extra accessories (25%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 10,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Why impact sockets?

Impact sockets are made from tougher, more ductile steel (usually Cr-Mo) with thicker walls and a black finish. Chrome sockets can crack or shatter on an impact wrench.

1/2 or 3/8-inch drive?

1/2-inch drive is the standard for lug nuts, suspension and most impact wrenches. 3/8-inch fits smaller impact wrenches and tight spots.

Deep or shallow?

Deep sockets reach nuts on long studs and bolts. Shallow sockets fit where clearance is tight. A set with both covers most jobs.

Six-point or twelve-point?

Six-point impact sockets grip the flats of a fastener and are far less likely to round it off under impact.

Is a ratchet OK on impact sockets?

Yes, impact sockets work with hand ratchets. The reverse isn't true: don't use chrome sockets on an impact wrench.
