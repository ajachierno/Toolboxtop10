How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $50; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from metric sae (30%), flex head (25%), storage (25%), teeth 90 (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Why ratcheting wrenches?

A ratcheting box end lets you keep turning a bolt without lifting the wrench off and refitting it. It's much faster in tight spaces where a socket and ratchet won't fit.

72 or 90 teeth?

More teeth means a smaller swing to catch the next tooth: 5 degrees for 72-tooth, about 4 degrees for 90-tooth. It matters in very cramped spots.

Fixed or flex head?

Flex heads pivot to reach bolts at awkward angles. Fixed heads are stronger and simpler. Many mechanics own both.

Metric, SAE or both?

Modern cars are almost all metric. Older American cars, trailers and many household fixtures use SAE. A combined set covers both.

Don't use it as a breaker bar

Ratcheting mechanisms aren't meant for breaking loose seized bolts. Break them free with a standard wrench or breaker bar, then spin them off with the ratchet.
