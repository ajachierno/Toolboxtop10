Buyer's guide

Gantry crane or engine hoist?

An engine hoist is cheaper ($170 to $300) and folds away, but its legs have to roll under the car and its boom loses capacity as it extends. A gantry crane straddles the car or bench, lifts straight up on a trolley, and holds its full rating across the beam. Gantries are better for repeated lifts, heavy diesels, and moving parts across the shop.

Will it fit in my garage?

Check the minimum height first. Most 3-ton gantries here start at 8.53 ft, too tall for a standard 8 ft garage ceiling. The JEGS (7 ft 11 in.) and both Yannivia kits (7.65 and 7.78 ft) are built to fit under 8 ft. Remember the hoist and chain hang below the beam, so your lift height is less than the crane height.

Frame only or a complete kit?

Frame-only gantries need a trolley (which rides the I-beam) and a chain hoist, bought separately. The Yannivia and LYFOO kits include both, plus slings and shackles. BSMTEK, Dkenicor, LDHTHOPI, and VEVOR include a trolley on some listings but not a hoist.

How much capacity do I need?

A 1-ton (2,000 lb) gantry handles most car engines and transmissions. Diesels, truck engines with transmissions attached, and machine tools call for 2 to 3 tons. Keep the hoist and trolley ratings at or above the frame's rating; the system is only as strong as its weakest part.

Can I move it with a load on it?

If the manual allows moving under load at all, keep it to short moves on smooth, level concrete with the load low and centered. Lock the casters before lifting, and never roll a loaded gantry over cracks, expansion joints, or slopes.
