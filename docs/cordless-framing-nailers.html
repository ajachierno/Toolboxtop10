Buyer's guide

21-degree or 30-degree?

It comes down to the nails you can buy locally and your building code. 21° nailers take plastic-strip full round head nails. 30° (and 30-34°) nailers take paper-tape nails, usually clipped head, and the magazine is shorter so it fits between studs more easily. Check that full round head nails aren't required where you build before going 30° with clipped heads.

Are cordless framing nailers as good as pneumatic?

For decks, sheds, remodels and punch-out work, yes: no compressor, no hose, and the better ones fire 2 to 3 nails per second. Full-time production framers still tend to use pneumatic because the tools are lighter and never need charging.

Why are these so heavy?

A cordless framer carries its own motor and drive system, so most weigh 8 to 11 lb before you add the battery. The Metabo HPT kits are the lightest here at 8.2 to 8.4 lb.

Should I buy the bare tool or a kit?

If you already own batteries on the same platform, buy the bare tool and use your largest battery. If not, a kit is usually the cheaper way in than buying a bare tool, battery and charger separately.

How many nails per charge?

The listings here claim about 500 to 1,100 per charge, depending on the battery. Use a 4.0Ah or larger battery for framing; small compact packs run out fast and can struggle to sink 3-1/2 inch nails.
