How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $25; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from pneumatic (30%), tool tray (25%), capacity 300 (25%), five casters (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 7,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Pneumatic or fixed height?

A pneumatic stool adjusts with a lever, usually 15 to 20 inches, which suits brake jobs and bumper work. Fixed creeper seats sit lower and cost less.

How many casters?

Five casters are more stable than four, especially when you lean. Bigger casters roll over cords and cracks more easily.

Do I need a tool tray?

A tray under the seat keeps sockets and fasteners with you as you roll around the car. It's one of the most useful features.

Capacity

Choose a stool rated comfortably above your weight. Most mechanic stools are rated 250 or 300 lb.

Backrest or not?

Low stools for wheel work rarely have backs. For long sessions at a workbench, a taller stool with a backrest is more comfortable.
