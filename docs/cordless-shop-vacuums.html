How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $100; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from hepa (35%), a blower port (25%), a brushless motor (20%), a complete kit (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 10,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Do cordless shop vacs have enough suction?

For spills, car interiors, and jobsite dust, yes. Compact cordless vacs run roughly 35 to 57 CFM; plug-in shop vacs of similar size quote more. If you need to pull water out of a flooded basement, the 9-gallon EGO (110 CFM, 100 in. of water lift) is the only cordless pick here that's in that range.

Should I buy a bare tool or a kit?

If you already own DEWALT, Milwaukee, CRAFTSMAN, Makita, or EGO batteries, buy the bare vac on the same platform and save the battery cost. If you own none, a kit like the Seesii or WORX costs less than buying a name-brand bare vac plus a battery and charger.

How long does a battery last in a shop vac?

Vacs drain batteries fast. Listings on this page quote 20 to 30 minutes on a compact vac at full power, up to 60 minutes with a 5.0Ah battery on the Makita, and up to 60 minutes in ECO mode on the EGO with two 6.0Ah packs. Plan on a spare battery for anything bigger than a car.

What do CFM, water lift, and kPa mean?

CFM is airflow, which moves light debris like sawdust. Water lift (in inches) and kPa both measure sealed suction, which lifts heavy debris and water. Brands publish different ones, so compare like with like: the Makita lists 57 CFM and 27 in. of lift; the Milwaukee M18 FUEL lists 50 CFM and 47 in.

Do I need a HEPA filter?

For drywall, concrete, and sanding dust, yes; a regular filter lets fine dust blow back out the exhaust. Most vacs here ship with a HEPA-rated filter. The EGO and WORX use cartridge filters that aren't listed as HEPA.

Can a cordless vac double as a dust extractor for my sander?

The Makita XCV11Z is sold as a dust extractor and has a filter-cleaning system for that job. The others can connect to a tool's dust port with an adapter, but the compact tanks fill quickly.

Gisam

Cordless Shop Vac, 3000W Brushless Motor, 2x4.0Ah Battery Powered 3-in-1 Wet Dry Vacuum with HEPA Filter

3.1 8 reviews   $109.99

The headline numbers don't hold up. A 21V battery vac can't draw 3000 watts, the suction figures contradict each other, and the copy describes a different, heavier vac. The Seesii kit costs the same and makes believable claims.

Why we'd skip it

  • The title claims a 3000W motor, but the listing gives 21 volts and two 4.0Ah batteries. 3000W at 21V would pull about 140 amps, far beyond what those packs can deliver.
  • It lists 915 kPa for "daily use" and 25 kPa for "heavy-duty". The everyday mode can't be 36 times stronger than the heavy mode; 915 kPa is about nine atmospheres.
  • The spec table says the vac weighs 3 lb, while the bullet text says "At only 8lbs".
  • The bullets repeat the Seesii listing nearly word for word, so it's unclear which specs describe this product.
