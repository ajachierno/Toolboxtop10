How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $95; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (30%), speed control (30%), battery included (20%), an electric blade brake (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What is a die grinder for?

Grinding, porting, deburring, cleaning welds and cutting with small bits and wheels on a 1/4-inch shank. Think of it as a heavy-duty rotary tool.

Straight or right angle?

Straight die grinders reach into holes and pipes. Right-angle grinders get into tight engine bays and corners where a straight body won't fit.

Cordless vs. air?

Air die grinders need a big compressor running constantly. Cordless models like Milwaukee's M12 FUEL are built to replace them for most shop work, without a hose.

Why a brake?

A brake stops the bit quickly when you release the switch, so you can set the tool down sooner and it's less likely to grab.

What speed?

Up to 25,000 RPM is typical. Lower speeds suit larger wheels and softer metals; variable speed lets you match the bit.
