How we rank

The list is ordered by that score. The features score is built from safety valve (30%), certified tank (30%), gauge (20%), push button (20%), capped at 100. Buyer's guide

Tank or handheld?

A 5-gallon tank holds the most air and seats big truck and tractor tires best. You set it down and aim the barrel. Handheld blasters (2 to 3 gallons) have a trigger and are easier to aim one-handed, but they hold less air, which matters on wide or stiff tires.

What compressor do I need?

Any shop compressor that reaches 116 to 150 psi will fill a bead seater; tank size only changes how long it takes to fill. Fill it to the operating pressure on the label (most here say 85 to 116 psi), not the maximum.

Is a bead seater safe?

It's a pressure vessel, so treat it like one. Wear eye and ear protection, never exceed the rated pressure, and buy a tank with a safety valve and a certification (ASME or CE) you can check. Remove the valve core first so the tire takes the blast, then reinstall it and inflate normally.

Why won't my tire seat even with a blaster?

Lube the bead and rim with tire mounting lubricant, remove the valve core, and make sure the bead is pushed into the center of the rim before you blast. Very stiff or wide tires may need a ratchet strap around the tread to push the beads outward first.

What size tires can it handle?

Most 5-gallon tanks here are rated for tires up to 24.5 in. (semi-truck size). The VEVOR 2.1 gal and XtremepowerUS 12L handhelds are rated to 22.5 in. The OEMTOOLS lists the widest range, 4 to 24.5 in.
