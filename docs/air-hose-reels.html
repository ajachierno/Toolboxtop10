How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $80; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from hose included (30%), auto rewind (30%), steel reel (20%), guide arm (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 3,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Retractable or manual?

Spring-retractable reels pull the hose back with a tug; they're the most convenient for a garage. Manual crank reels are cheaper, hold longer hoses, and suit people who already own hose.

What hose length do I need?

Measure from the reel to the farthest point you'll work, then add 10 feet. 50 ft covers a two-car garage from a wall mount; 65 ft and up suits bigger shops or driveways.

Hybrid or rubber hose?

Hybrid polymer hose is light and kink-resistant. Rubber hose is heavier but lays flat and stays supple in the cold. Flexzilla's hose is famously flexible.

Where should I mount it?

On a wall or ceiling stud near the compressor, with a short lead-in hose from the compressor to the reel. Use the swivel bracket so the hose pays out in any direction.

Is 3/8-inch hose enough?

Yes for nailers, impact wrenches and inflating tires. 1/2-inch hose is only worth it for long runs to high-demand tools.
