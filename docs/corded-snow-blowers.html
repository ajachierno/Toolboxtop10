How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $140; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from wide path (30%), steel auger (25%), deep intake (20%), long throw (15%), led lights (10%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Corded or cordless snow blower?

A corded blower never runs out of battery and costs less: the 20-22 in blowers here run $140-$250, against $270 and up for a battery blower of similar width with its pack. The trade-off is the cord. You're limited to how far an extension cord reaches, and you have to keep it clear of the auger.

What extension cord do I need?

An outdoor-rated cord in the gauge and length your blower's manual specifies. Snow Joe says not to go over 100 ft. A cord that's too long or too thin starves a 15 A motor, so buy the heaviest gauge the manual allows.

Snow shovel or snow blower?

An electric snow shovel (10-16 in) is a handheld auger you push like a shovel. The ones here weigh 12-16 lb and suit steps, decks and walks after a few inches. A walk-behind blower (20-22 in) rolls on wheels, takes 10-13 in of snow per pass and clears a driveway much faster.

Does the auger material matter?

A single-stage blower's auger scrapes the pavement. A steel or rubber-tipped steel auger stands up to packed snow and ice chunks better than plastic. Westinghouse and DEKOPRO list steel augers; several other listings don't say, and Snow Joe's SJ627E listing says both.

How deep a snowfall can a corded blower handle?

The listed intake depth is the limit for one pass: 6-8 in for the snow shovels, 10-13 in for the blowers here (PowerSmart lists 6 in). For deeper or wet, heavy snow, take it in two passes or clear it before it piles up. The plowed-in pile at the end of the driveway is two-stage gas territory.
