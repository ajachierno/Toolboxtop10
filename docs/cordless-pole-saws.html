How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $100; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from reach 12 (30%), battery included (25%), detaches (25%), auto oil (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How much reach do I need?

Add your height and arm reach (about 7 to 8 ft) to the shaft length. A 9 to 10 ft pole reaches limbs around 15 to 16 ft off the ground; 13 to 16 ft poles get you higher. Never cut directly overhead.

Pole saw or 2-in-1?

Dedicated pole saws balance better on the pole. 2-in-1 models detach into a mini chainsaw for ground cleanup, which is handy for small yards.

What bar size?

6 to 8 inches handles branches up to about 4 to 6 inches. 10-inch bars like the EGO and WORX cut a bit thicker.

Why does auto oiling matter?

The chain needs bar oil. Auto oilers feed it while you cut; without one you'll oil by hand and wear chains faster.

Is a pole saw safe?

Wear eye protection and a hard hat, stand to the side of the branch, and stay at least 10 feet from power lines. Hire a pro for limbs near wires or over the house.
