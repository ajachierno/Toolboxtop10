How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $300; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from helical (35%), cast iron (25%), width 8 (20%), carbide (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,200 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Jointer or planer?

A jointer flattens one face and squares one edge. A planer then makes the opposite face parallel at a set thickness. For rough lumber you need both, or a planer sled.

How wide?

6 inches handles edges and narrow boards. 8 to 12 inches lets you flatten wider boards for tabletops without ripping them first.

Spiral or straight knives?

Spiral (helical) heads cut with a shearing action, so they're quieter and tear out less on figured wood. Carbide inserts last much longer than HSS.

Why table length matters?

Longer tables straighten longer boards. Benchtop jointers work best on boards up to about twice the table length.

Safety

Always use push blocks, never joint pieces shorter than about 12 inches, and keep the guard in place.
