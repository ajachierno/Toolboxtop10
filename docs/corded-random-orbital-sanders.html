Buyer's guide

What does "random orbital" mean?

The pad spins in a circle and orbits off-center at the same time, so it never traces the same path twice. That random motion is what stops it from leaving the circular swirl marks a fixed rotary sander leaves, which is why it's the go-to for finishing wood before paint or stain.

Do I need variable speed?

Variable speed lets you slow the pad down for delicate work like veneer, paint prep, or polishing, where full speed can burn or gouge, and speed it up for fast stock removal. If you only sand bare wood, a single-speed tool like the DEWALT DWE6421K or BLACK+DECKER is fine. For mixed materials, pay for the dial.

Dust bag, canister, or shop vac?

A cloth dust bag is convenient but fills fast and lets fine dust through. A cyclonic box or twist-off canister (SKIL, Bosch) is tidier and shows the fill level. The cleanest option is a tool with a vacuum port, like the DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, or Metabo HPT picks, hooked to a shop vac — it collects the most and keeps the air clearer.

Is the 5-inch pad standard?

Yes. Five-inch hook-and-loop discs are the most common and cheapest sandpaper you can buy, and every tool on this list uses them. Match the disc's hole pattern (usually 8 holes) to the pad so the dust extraction lines up.

Corded or cordless?

A corded sander gives constant power for long sanding sessions and costs much less, since there's no battery to buy. Cordless only makes sense if you need to sand where a cord can't reach or you're already invested in a battery platform.

What do the OPM numbers mean?

OPM is orbits per minute — how fast the pad moves. Higher OPM removes material faster; most 5-inch sanders top out around 12,000 to 13,000 OPM. On a variable-speed tool, the low end matters just as much: it's what lets you dial back for a fine finish.

Tools to avoid

Not everything in the search results deserves your money. This one would rank dead last on our scale — here's the listing to walk past, and exactly why.

✕ Avoid
ZFE

ZFE Random Orbital Sander 5" & 6" Pneumatic Palm Sander

4.4 2,126 reviews   $46.99

This isn't an electric sander. It's a pneumatic (air) tool that does nothing on its own — it needs an air compressor most buyers don't have, yet it sits in the results right beside plug-in sanders. If you want to plug in and sand, any of the corded tools above is the right buy.

Why we'd skip it

  • Its power source is pneumatic: it runs on compressed air, not electricity, and has no cord or plug. It won't turn on without an air compressor, which is not included and typically costs $100 to $300 more than the sander.
  • The listing buries that. It's titled simply "Random Orbital Sander," ranks alongside corded electric sanders, and even lists a "1 A" amperage and a "10,000 rpm" spec — figures that make no sense for an air tool and make it easy to mistake for an electric one.
  • A palm air sander like this needs a steady 5 to 7 CFM at 90 PSI to run continuously. Many small pancake and hot-dog home compressors can't sustain that, so even a buyer who owns one may find the tool starves and slows under load.
  • It's a 5"/6" combo that needs backing-plate swaps, and 6" discs are less common and pricier than the 5" hook-and-loop discs every electric sander above uses.
