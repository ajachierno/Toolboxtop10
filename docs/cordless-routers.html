How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $85; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (25%), a complete kit (25%), variable speed (20%), a soft start (15%), an LED work light (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 10,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Is a cordless router as good as a corded one?

For compact-router work (edges, hinges, laminate), yes: the best cordless routers match a corded trim router and you skip the cord catching on the workpiece. For big bits and long dados, a corded full-size router or DEWALT's cordless plunge router has more power.

Do I need brushless?

Brushless motors get more cutting per charge and hold speed better under load. On a battery tool that matters more than on a corded one.

Kit or bare tool?

Most brand-name cordless routers are sold bare, for people already on a platform. If you don't own batteries, a budget kit gets you running for under $85, but its batteries won't fit other brands' tools.

What speed range do I need?

For 1/4-inch edge bits, 20,000 to 30,000 rpm is plenty. A wider range down to 10,000 rpm lets you slow down for larger profile bits without burning.

Can I put a cordless router in a router table?

Compact routers can go in small trim-router tables, but battery runtime makes them a poor fit for long table sessions. A corded full-size router is the better table router.
