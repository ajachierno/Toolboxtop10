How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $80 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from electronic (45%), auto measure (20%), alu modes (20%), warranty (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

Bubble balancer or computerized balancer?

A bubble balancer (about $67 to $76) shows static imbalance: the heavy spot that makes a wheel hop. It works well for narrow car tires and occasional use. A computerized spin balancer ($650 and up) also measures dynamic imbalance, the side-to-side wobble that shows up on wide tires and at highway speed, and tells you exactly how much weight to add on each side of the rim.

What does ±1 g accuracy mean?

The balancer can detect imbalance down to about 1 gram (0.04 oz). Wheel weights are sold in 1/4 oz (7 g) steps, so ±1 g is more precise than you can correct with standard weights. Most computerized balancers on this list claim ±1 g; a few listings give no figure.

Will a bubble balancer fit my truck?

The bubble balancers here fit hubs from 1-1/2 to 4 in. across, which covers most cars and light trucks. Check your wheel's center bore before buying. Heavy-duty truck wheels need a full-size machine like the KATOOL KT-B760, which takes rims up to 30 in.

What is ALU mode?

ALU modes tell the balancer you're using stick-on weights on the inside of an alloy rim instead of clip-on weights on the rim edge, so it calculates weight positions for those spots. The I40 PRO, the KATOOL KT-B700 and the XK 690 list ALU modes.

What else do I need?

Wheel weights (clip-on for steel rims, adhesive for alloys), a weight hammer or pliers, and a way to get the tire off the car. A computerized balancer also needs cones that fit your wheel's center bore; most include a basic set.
