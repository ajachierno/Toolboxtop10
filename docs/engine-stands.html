How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $80; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from high capacity (30%), folding (25%), locking casters (20%), self locking (15%), tool tray (10%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How much capacity do I need?

Go by the dressed engine weight, then leave margin. Four-cylinders and most V6s fit on a 750 lb stand. A small-block V8 with heads and intake is often 500 to 600 lb, and big-blocks and diesels run heavier, so a 1,000 to 1,500 lb stand is the safer pick. Stands are often loaded off-center, and that's when the rating matters.

Three casters or four or more?

Three-caster T-base stands are cheap and common, but they tip more easily when you roll a loaded engine over a crack or bump. H-base and U-base stands with four or five casters are steadier. The 1,500 lb VEVOR and the Big Red T26801 both use five.

Does a folding stand matter?

Only if you're short on space. Folding stands like the VEVOR 1,500 lb (14.8 in. folded), Big Red T26801, and Maxority stand against a wall between projects. Fixed-frame stands are slightly simpler and usually cheaper.

Will it fit my engine?

The four adjustable arms fit most domestic and foreign blocks, but check bolt size and length for your engine. Many people buy grade 8 bolts in the right thread so the arms clamp to the block's bellhousing bolt pattern securely.

Why does self-locking rotation matter?

On a basic stand you pull a pin, rotate, and re-pin; the engine can swing if it's off-balance. A self-locking or geared head, like the Jackco's, holds the engine at any angle, which is safer with a heavy, top-heavy block.
