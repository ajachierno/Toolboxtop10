The list is ordered by that score. Buyer's guide

Is a cordless heat gun as strong as a corded one?

No. A corded heat gun draws 1,200 to 2,000 watts from the wall; the third-party battery guns here are rated at 350 watts, and battery packs can't sustain corded-level output for long. Cordless guns are for shrink tubing, small vinyl and plastic jobs, and spots with no outlet. For stripping a door or a window frame, see our corded heat gun ranking.

How long does a battery last?

Shorter than you'd guess. DEWALT lists up to 42 minutes for the DCE530, CRAFTSMAN up to 15 minutes, and Makita 21 minutes on high with a 6.0Ah pack. The 350-watt third-party guns list about 6 minutes on a 2.0Ah pack and about 12 on a 4.0Ah. Use the largest battery you own, and keep a second one charged for longer jobs.

Should I buy the brand-name gun or a battery-compatible one?

If you use it often, buy the gun from the company that makes your batteries: it's built for that pack, and DEWALT, Milwaukee, RYOBI, CRAFTSMAN, and Makita back their own tools. The compatible guns from LIVOWALNY and Edtran cost a third to a half as much and add adjustable heat, which makes them reasonable for occasional shrink-tube and craft work. Their makers don't share a warranty with the battery brand.

Do I need adjustable temperature?

For vinyl wrap, thin plastic, and anything that scorches, yes. The DEWALT, Milwaukee, RYOBI, and CRAFTSMAN listings give a single maximum temperature, from 875 to 990°F, with no low setting. The Makita has variable temperature, and the third-party guns have two settings or a digital range.

What are the nozzles for?

A reflector wraps heat around shrink tubing and pipe, a concentrator or reduction nozzle focuses it on a small spot, a flat or wide-slot nozzle spreads it along a strip of paint or adhesive, and a glass protector keeps heat off window panes. The Milwaukee 2688-20 is the only gun here that ships without one.

Is it safe to leave the battery on?

Take it off when you're done. The LIVOWALNY listings warn that the gun drains the battery if it's left attached. Set the gun down on a non-flammable surface until the nozzle cools; it stays hot after you release the trigger.
