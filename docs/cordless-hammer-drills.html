How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $80; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from a brushless motor (30%), a complete kit (30%), a 2-speed gearbox (20%), an LED work light (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Do I need a hammer drill or a regular drill?

A hammer drill does everything a drill/driver does, plus a hammer mode for brick, block and the odd concrete anchor hole. If you ever hang things on masonry, it's worth the small price difference. For lots of concrete, look at a rotary hammer instead.

Kit or bare tool?

If you already own batteries for a platform, buy the bare tool and save the cost of a battery and charger. If you don't, a kit is cheaper than buying the pieces separately, and the platform you choose decides which tools you'll buy later.

Is brushless worth paying for?

Brushless motors run cooler, get more work from each charge and last longer, because there are no carbon brushes to wear out. For occasional home use a brushed drill is fine; for regular masonry work, brushless is worth it.

What do torque and BPM mean?

Torque (in-lb or UWO) is twisting force, which matters for big bits and long screws. BPM is blows per minute in hammer mode, which matters for how fast it drills masonry. Brands measure torque differently, so compare within a brand rather than across them.

Can a cordless hammer drill handle concrete?

It can drill anchor holes up to about 1/2 inch in concrete, slowly. If you're drilling more than a few holes, or larger ones, a rotary hammer with SDS-Plus bits will be much faster and easier on your wrist.
