Buyer's guide

What is orbital action, and do I need it?

Orbital action swings the blade forward into the cut on the upstroke instead of just moving straight up and down. Higher orbital settings cut faster and rougher, which is great for ripping through softwood; turn it off for slow, clean cuts in hardwood, metal, or finish work. Most jigsaws here offer four settings so you can match the material.

How many amps do I need?

5 amps handles plywood, trim, and general DIY cutting. 6 to 6.5 amps is the sweet spot for most work, giving you power without bogging down in thicker or harder stock. 7 amps, like the Bosch JS470E, is for frequent or heavy cutting where you don't want the motor to slow under load.

What does tool-free blade change mean?

A tool-free clamp lets you pop a blade in or out by hand or with a lever, no wrench or hex key needed. It sounds minor until you change blades ten times in a project. The DEWALT, both Bosch tools, Makita, SKIL, HYCHIKA, and WORKPRO have it; the BLACK+DECKER and CRAFTSMAN need a small tool.

T-shank or U-shank blades?

T-shank (tang) blades are the modern standard and snap into tool-free clamps, with by far the widest selection at any store. U-shank is the older style with a slot. Every tool on this list takes T-shank blades, so buy those and you'll never hunt for a match.

Do I need variable speed and bevel cuts?

Variable speed lets you slow down for metal, laminate, and tight curves and speed up for straight cuts in wood, so it's worth having — every pick here includes it. A bevel base tilts the shoe up to 45 degrees for angled cuts; the HYCHIKA and WORKPRO tilt both directions, the rest go one way to 45.

Corded or cordless?

A corded jigsaw gives constant power for long or heavy cuts and costs far less, with no battery to buy or recharge. Cordless is worth it only if you need to cut where a cord can't reach or you're already on a battery platform.
