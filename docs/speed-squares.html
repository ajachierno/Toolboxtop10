How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is the top-scoring pick with at least 4.0 stars and 30 reviews (a ready-to-use kit wins a close call); Best Budget is the top score at or under $10 that costs less than Best Overall; and Money No Object is the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from aluminum (30%), scribe notches (25%), rafter guide (25%), multi pack (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 200 or more reviews gets full marks. A listing under two years old with at least 30 reviews has its count scaled up for the shorter time it has had to collect them, never more than double. Buyer's guide

What's a speed square for?

Marking square and 45-degree lines on boards, guiding a circular saw for square cuts, and laying out rafter angles. It's the most-used layout tool on a framing job.

7 or 12 inch?

The 7-inch fits a tool belt and covers 2x lumber. A 12-inch square spans wider boards and is better for stair stringers and wide rafters.

Die-cast or laser-etched?

Die-cast markings are molded into the square and can't wear off. Laser-etched markings are crisp but can fade with heavy use.

What are scribe notches?

Notches at set intervals let you hold a pencil in place and slide the square along a board to draw a line parallel to the edge.

Do I need the Blue Book?

Only for rafters. It explains how to use the degree and pitch scales to mark common, hip, valley and jack rafters.
