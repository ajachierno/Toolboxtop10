How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $120; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from large throat (30%), tool-free adjustments (25%), arm lift (25%), light (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 5,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What size scroll saw do I need?

The throat (16, 20 or 22 inches) is the distance from the blade to the back of the arm, which limits how wide a piece you can turn. 16 inches handles most hobby work; 20-22 inches suits large fretwork, signs and panels.

Pinned or pinless blades?

Pinless blades are thinner and thread through tiny starter holes for fine inside cuts. Pinned blades are easier to change but need bigger holes. Most saws here take pinless blades; some take both.

Why does a lifting arm matter?

For inside cuts you drill a hole, thread the blade through, and reattach it. An upper arm that lifts and locks makes that quick, which matters on pieces with dozens of cutouts.

Tilting table or tilting arm?

Most saws tilt the table for bevel cuts. Pro saws like the BUCKTOOL and JET tilt the arm instead, so the workpiece stays flat and easier to control.

What speed should I use?

Slower speeds for thin wood, plastic and metal (to avoid melting and burning); faster speeds for thick hardwood. Variable speed lets you dial it in.
