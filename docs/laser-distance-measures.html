How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $30; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from accuracy 1 16 (30%), long range (25%), angle or app (25%), green beam (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 20,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How accurate are laser measures?

Most here are rated ±1/16 inch, as good as a careful tape measure, over their full range. Klein rates its 100 ft model ±1/16 inch to 30 ft. Accuracy drops on shiny, dark or angled surfaces; put a card or tape on glass.

How much range do I need?

Indoors, 100 ft is plenty for almost any room. 165 ft covers big rooms and halls. 300 to 400 ft matters outdoors, where you'll need a target, a green beam or Bosch's camera viewfinder to see the dot.

Red or green beam?

Green dots look up to 4 times brighter than red, so they're easier to see in sunlit rooms and outdoors. They cost more and use more battery.

Can it calculate square footage?

Most here do area (two measurements multiplied), volume and Pythagorean height. That's handy for flooring and paint estimates.

Watch out for 'bidirectional' range

Some dual-laser models advertise the two directions added together, so a '656 ft' meter reaches 328 ft each way. Check the single-direction range.
