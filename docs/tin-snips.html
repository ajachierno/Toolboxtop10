How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $15; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from multi cut set (30%), capacity 18ga (30%), one hand latch (20%), a lifetime warranty (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 8,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

Which color do I need?

Aviation snips are color-coded. Yellow cuts straight and wide curves, red cuts left curves, green cuts right curves. If you buy one, get yellow. For ductwork and flashing, a full set saves fighting the metal.

What does 18 gauge mean?

Gauge is sheet thickness; a bigger number is thinner. 18-gauge steel is about 1.2 mm, the upper end for hand snips. Residential HVAC duct is typically in the mid-20s gauge.

What are offset snips?

Offset snips have the handles raised above the blades, so your hand stays clear of the sheet on long cuts. Pros cutting lots of duct and roofing prefer them.

Can I cut stainless?

Most snips here cut about 22-gauge stainless, which is harder than mild steel. Check the listing's stainless rating.

How do I avoid cuts on my hands?

Wear cut-resistant gloves. Sheet metal edges are razor sharp, and the waste strip curls toward you.
