How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $45; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from depth adjust (25%), selectable trigger (25%), a carrying case (20%), dry fire lockout (15%), no mar tip (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What's a narrow crown stapler for?

18-gauge narrow crown staples (1/4-inch crown) hold better than brads in thin material: cabinet backs, drawer bottoms, upholstery frames, trim backing and underlayment.

Staple length

Pick staples about 2-1/2 times the thickness of the material you're fastening. Most staplers take 1/2 to 1-1/2 or 1-5/8 inch staples.

Contact or sequential trigger?

Sequential fires one staple per trigger pull for accuracy. Contact (bump) fires when you press the nose down with the trigger held, which is faster for production work.

2-in-1 or dedicated?

2-in-1 tools shoot brads and staples, good for DIYers. Dedicated staplers are lighter and more reliable for heavy use.

Pneumatic or cordless?

Pneumatic staplers are lighter and cheaper but need a compressor. Cordless ones go anywhere but are heavier and cost more.
