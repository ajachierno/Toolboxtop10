How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $55; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from long pins (30%), oil free (20%), dry fire lockout (20%), depth adjust (15%), a carrying case (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 2,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What is a pin nailer for?

A 23-gauge pinner shoots headless pins so thin the holes almost disappear. Use it to hold trim, moldings and glued parts while the glue dries. Pins have little holding power on their own.

Pin nailer or brad nailer?

Brad nailers (18 gauge) hold better and are for trim you won't glue. Pin nailers leave smaller holes and won't split thin stock. Many woodworkers own both.

How long a pin do I need?

About twice the thickness of the piece you're attaching. 1-3/8 inch covers most trim. For hardwood face frames and thicker parts, 2-inch pinners like the DEWALT DWFP2350K help.

Oil or oil-free?

Oil-free nailers like the DEWALT and Arrow won't spit oil onto finished wood. Oiled nailers need a few drops in the air inlet daily.

What compressor do I need?

Almost any. Pinners use very little air; a small pancake compressor at 70 to 100 PSI is plenty.
