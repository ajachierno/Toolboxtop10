How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $10; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from gfci test (25%), lcd voltage (25%), open fault detect (20%), certified (15%), extra function (15%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 10,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What does an outlet tester show?

Plug it in and the lights or screen show whether the outlet is wired correctly or has an open ground, open neutral, open hot, or reversed hot and neutral.

Do I need a GFCI test button?

Yes for kitchens, bathrooms, garages and outdoors. The button leaks a small current to ground so you can confirm the GFCI actually trips.

What can't it detect?

Basic testers can miss a hot-ground reverse combined with an open ground, and they can't judge connection quality. They're a quick check, not a full inspection.

LCD or lights?

Light codes are cheap and fine for a yes/no. An LCD shows actual voltage, which helps spot low-voltage problems and makes faults easier to read.

What about AFCI?

Newer homes use arc-fault breakers in bedrooms and living areas. Only testers like the Klein RT310 can simulate an arc fault to check them.
