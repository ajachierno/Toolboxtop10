How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $140; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from resaw (30%), a 2-speed gearbox (25%), bearings (25%), fence (20%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 1,500 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

What size band saw do I need?

9- and 10-inch benchtop saws cut curves, small parts and stock up to about 3-1/2 to 4-3/4 inches thick. 14-inch floor saws resaw boards 8 to 13 inches tall and run wider blades for straight rips.

What is resawing?

Resawing slices a board on edge into thinner boards or veneer. It needs a tall cut depth, a strong motor, a wide blade and a tall fence, which is why it's a 14-inch saw's job.

Why two speeds?

High speed for wood; low speed for plastics, aluminum and other non-ferrous metals, which melt or dull blades at wood speeds.

Which blade should I use?

Narrow blades (1/8-1/4 inch) cut tight curves; wide blades (1/2 inch and up) cut straighter and resaw. Fewer teeth per inch cut faster; more teeth cut smoother.

Do ball-bearing guides matter?

Yes. Bearing guides above and below the table keep the blade from drifting and twisting, which gives straighter cuts and longer blade life.
