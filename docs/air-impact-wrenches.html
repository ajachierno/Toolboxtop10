1/2-inch pneumatic impact guns for lug nuts, suspension and seized bolts, ranked from live Amazon data.

How we rank

Every tool gets one score from 0 to 100, weighted 45% on its star rating, 35% on how many people have reviewed it (more reviews = more confidence the rating is real), and 20% on the features that matter for the job. The list is ordered by that score. Best Overall is our pick for the best all-around, ready-to-use tool; Best Budget the best value at or under $60; and Money No Object the one to buy if price is no object.

The features score is built from twin hammer (35%), light (35%), high torque (30%), capped at 100. The review-count score is on a log scale, so each tenfold jump in reviews adds the same amount, and 4,000 or more reviews gets full marks. Open Why this score on any tool to see its numbers.

Buyer's guide

How much torque do I need?

Lug nuts are torqued to about 80-150 ft-lbs, and 500-600 ft-lbs of breakaway torque handles almost all car work. Big numbers help with rusted suspension and axle nuts. Budget brands' torque claims often exceed those of the best pro guns, so treat them as optimistic.

What compressor do I need for an impact wrench?

Most 1/2-inch guns need 4 to 6 CFM at 90 PSI. A 20-gallon or larger compressor keeps up with tire work; small pancake compressors run out of air quickly.

What is a twin-hammer mechanism?

Two hammers strike the anvil each revolution, giving smoother, balanced blows and more torque for the size. It's the common design for shop-grade guns.

Should I torque lug nuts with an impact wrench?

No. Run them on at a low setting, then finish with a torque wrench to the vehicle's spec. Over-tightening warps rotors and stretches studs.

Do air tools need oil?

Yes. Put a few drops of air tool oil in the inlet before each use, or use an inline oiler.
